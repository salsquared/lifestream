LIFEstream Bible
A fusion-powered biopunk death-race thriller.
By Salvador Salcedo 


Logline 
In a fusion-powered future built on disposable lives, a grieving outlaw pilot lands a seat in the deadliest sport ever staged: three races climbing the side of a space elevator to the edge of orbit, flown at speeds and g-loads that knock a body unconscious — where most at the start line won't live to the finish line, and surviving the season is the only victory that counts.


Themes
Anti-capitalist. Dissociative. Extractive. Post-pandemic. Disposable. Neo-industrial. Post-traumatic. Surveillance-saturated. Monopolistic. Medicated. Gilded. Hollowed-out.


Anti-corporate / Anti-capitalist
The megacorporation is the defining villain of the LIFEstream world. MEGACORP's willingness to sacrifice 555 crew members to maximize Project Athena's return is the thesis statement: human life is a line item.
Dissociative
The texture of the world itself. The drift between what's real and what's broadcast, what's lived and what's received, what your body is doing and what your senses are reporting. LIFEstream is the technology of dissociation made commercial. Drops induce it pharmacologically. Even grief in this world arrives at a delay.
Extractive
Disaster Ridge to lithium, lithium to fusion fuel, fusion fuel to space elevator, space elevator to asteroid mining. Every layer of the economy is a drill bit pointed at something - the earth, the moon, the asteroid belt, the worker, the consumer.
Fusion-punk
A new sub-genre where fusion energy is the gilded gold of the age. Helium-3 is the new oil, the Moon is the new Persian Gulf, and ET1 is the supertanker route written into the sky. Every plot traces back to one question: who owns the flame?
Post-pandemic / Post-traumatic
X did not live through Black Fever, but he carries it. The world he inherits is scar tissue civilization - its laws, medications, and entertainments all exist because of deaths he never witnessed.
Neo-industrial
Star City rising from a literal wound in the earth. Lab-grown muscle bolted to metal endoskeletons. Brains grown in vats to compute the cure. The aesthetic of building anything at any cost.
Surveillance-saturated
MEGACORP read the crew's neural telemetry through their suits for years before using it to replace them. Black Fever screening became person-tracking. Everybody is a data source.
Monopolistic
Sanctioned by the UEA, not stolen from it. The tiered megacorp system gives each H-Mega exclusive legal rights over an entire manufacturing horizontal at planetary scale. Competition was outlawed in the name of efficiency, and monopoly was enshrined in international statute.
Medicated
Self-medication is the survival strategy of an entire generation. Drops are sold as a treatment but used as a coping mechanism. Adan synthesizes them by hand for X and the GZVA because the official supply was never going to be enough. To live in this world is to dose your way through it.
Gilded
The Helios Racing League dresses corporate sponsorship in the language of national pride. Project Athena was sold as humanity's voyage of the century. Every spectacle hides an extraction.
Disposable
People in this world are kept on staff only as long as they're operationally relevant for the elite. The Athena crew. Ines. The blue-collar worker the moment automata could replace them. Useful, then forgotten.
Hollowed-out
The cultural rot underneath everything else. A generation that survived Black Fever did so without their souls intact, and the generation born after inherited the void. LIFEstream, gambling, extreme sports, the racing league - all of them are popular because dopamine and adrenaline are the only things that still register. People don't seek extremes for thrill anymore; they seek them because nothing less can be felt at all.


Concepts
Births after 2020s
Today, people in general have children later than ever before. In 2025, the average was around 30 years old. It's not hard to believe that this trend will continue to 2060, around the time X and Adan are born, and beyond. So much so that by 2060 the average age to give birth is around 40 years old. 
We now know that it is a process related to how developed a country is. Development at a global scale can only be altered by a global event. Naturally this trend reversed slightly during the Black Fever Pandemic because so many people died. It soon went back to being extended however after 2046-2047. 
Among the changes in natal statistics, it had become popular in the 2030s for one to save their young sperm or egg in a lab. A soft social and completely voluntary eugenics culture among wealthy men and women. This only helped women to further extend the age at which they typically have children. They could make that decision to wait for the future with more confidence as so many did. Both Lazaro and Ines were part of this movement and participated in it before ever knowing each other. For people like them it was even more common to put off building a family of their own over the need to achieve in their careers. 
All these factors meant that Adan and X would be born in 2058 and 2062 respectively. At the time of their births Lazaro was 36 and 40, while Ines was 33 and 36. 


The Climate Era
The world of LIFEstream never lived through the COVID-19 pandemic. Without that crisis, the mRNA vaccine research that would later have armed humanity against the Black Fever Virus never matured beyond a niche field. National budgets through the late 2020s and 2030s consolidated instead around natural-disaster preparedness - the eventuality of major earthquakes, hurricanes, and most pressingly the rising seas already displacing coastal populations.
Climate Refugees
Climate displacement defined the era. Intensifying hurricanes, monsoons, and tornadoes drove people from disaster zones, but sea-level rise was the largest and most pervasive force. Coastal populations were uprooted by the millions, creating the largest refugee wave in modern history. By international agreement, countries were obligated to open their doors to displaced peoples without travel or customs restrictions - producing a level of cross-border movement not seen since the Second World War. Most refugees settled in Western nations, with significant additional flow into Russia, China, and the Gulf states. This open exodus would later be blamed for the spread of Black Fever, though the actual vector was elsewhere.
The Climate Change Attributable Disaster Insurance Fund (CCADIF)
The rising threat of climate litigation forced international action. In 2035, a UN-brokered statute established the Climate Change Attributable Disaster Insurance Fund - the largest cooperative international funding mechanism since the Marshall Plan. CCADIF assigned each country a percentage liability based on its lifetime atmospheric and nuclear pollution. (Rough early figures: United Kingdom 15%, United States 14.3%.) Receiver nations drew from the fund to rebuild after climate-attributable disasters. The Big One did not qualify - the United States was a principal contributor with sufficient internal FEMA capacity, and the earthquake predated the fund anyway.
CCADIF had a second, less obvious legacy. The fund created a special visa class for international assessors traveling to disaster zones. This same legal pathway later became the basis on which scientists trapped in foreign countries during the Black Fever Pandemic were granted citizenship and channeled into the underground labs of Project Xero - making CCADIF, almost incidentally, a piece of the infrastructure that would survive into Project Xero a decade later.


The Big One
Background
It had long been known that California was one of the most seismically active areas in the United States. Not so much so as Alaska, but making up more than 10% of the US population, it was a major concern for a major sect of the population. The state and Southern California had been preparing for decades for the eventuality of being struck by a large subterranean. Not having suffered a major magnitude earthquake beyond a 7.0 on the Richter Scale since the Great 1906 San Francisco earthquake, people knew a large magnitude earthquake was brewing underground. Every resident of consciousness, irrespective of age, had it in the back of their mind that any day, the Big One might strike. It just so happened to be over 100 years after the last, on July 10, 2034 at 8:04 am, that the Big One did eventually strike. 
Earthquake Specifics
Its epicenter was 16km underground along the San Andreas fault near the city of Mojave, California. An earthquake of previously unrecorded scale rattled South California to its core with a Richter-scale magnitude of 10.1, the highest ever recorded in the state. The towns and cities surrounding the epicenter would not survive the affair. Near the epicenter surface was at a massive chasm as a result of the San Andreas fault shearing and ripping open. Just moments later Mojave, the largest and closest city to the epicenter and the ridge burned to the ground. The locals who first discovered the chasm began to refer to it as “Disaster Ridge.”
Immediate Damage
With the majority of the population completely reliant on cars, the cities of Southern California were in their usual spur of activity getting children to school and adults to their places of work. The busiest time in all the day, the morning was the worst time The Big One could've struck. The roads and highways were jammed full of cars. The shaking was so violent that drivers were either forced to veer off the road to avoid collisions with lifted pieces of asphalt or newly formed ditches. Cars that were unlucky enough to be small and not weigh much were thrown up and down, side to side as the rumble pushed its way through the earth in all directions. Most buildings where the magnitude exceeded 8.0+ simply collapsed into themselves killing and injuring hundreds of thousands of Southern Californians. 
In downtown Los Angeles, all historic buildings made of brick before modern building codes cracked and fell from the force of the earth betraying their century of stability. The Italian Ball, Pico house, plaza Olvera, the 19th century chapel, all raised to their feet. The skyscrapers of old atop Bunker Hill and the surrounding downtown area ceased to exist. 
Near-Term Aftermath
Approximately a month after the earthquake, once the strongest side effects of the quake subsided, the United States Geological Survey and California Geological Survey put forth a grant to provide the top geologists from UCLA, USC and UC Berkeley, the career-defining and deeply personal task of exploring and cataloging this nascent geological feature that resulted in so much death and destruction. They first used the National Guard to clear the group of displaced peoples that took refuge near or inside the ridge and secured a perimeter.

Ines was 9 years old when The Big Ones hit. 
Personal Vehicles after the Big One
One of the largest logistical problems for a while was getting dead cars out of the streets. The streets were so out of order that it was very difficult for tow trucks to get all of them out of the roads. A new vehicle platform other than the wheel based one was needed to traverse the destroyed streets of South California. A robotics lab from USC was able to adapt and finish research on a large multipedal robotic platform. They originally sought to sell it as the basis for a new tank platform. When the Big One hit and the area had a huge logistics problem through the impassability of its streets, the scientists figured their transport platform could help pull cars through the new irregular terrain with more ease and speed than a traditional pick up tow truck. The state immediately approved to fund the adaptation work and the first few Spyder Tow Vehicles - 3 months after the disaster. The trial was an immediate success and the Tow Spyders could pull out cars out of the streets 5x times faster than traditional tow trucks. The success prompted the scientists to quickly patent and commercialize their work through a new for-profit corporate entity. The state ordered 200 Spyder Platforms from the company for the first year alone. The next year they wanted 400 more. 
Because of the massive road blockages caused by abandoned cars, the government decided to make all car driving by humans illegal. Cars made in California would now need to have an earthquake mode where they autonomously retreat out of the way whenever disasters strike. The technology at this point was well proven and most rich people had self driving vehicles. This new law made it so that it would be every car that needed it, increasing the minimum price of all new automobiles. Special permissions were given for legacy or historical vehicles but those were expensive and slow to get. In addition the laws made making and selling any gasoline or hybrid cars illegal - everything now had to be purely electric. After the Big One people ceased to drive themselves in the state of California. This state law propagated to other state assemblies and similar requirements were enacted on most of the west coast by 2040. 
The Death of Car Culture
Southern California's car culture, once one of the most distinctive in the world, vanished overnight with the human-driving ban. Modifications had been the heart of that culture - bigger engines, custom exhausts, turbos, lowrider hydraulics - and while the ban didn't outlaw the mods themselves, it made the point of them disappear. A car you could no longer drive ceased to be perceived as an extension of the self. The customization scene collapsed within a few years.
In its place, public transit expanded. Auto-driving taxis took over the personal-vehicle role for most short trips, and the LA Metro - which had been on a long, slow expansion arc through the 2020s - turned out to be remarkably resilient to earthquake damage. Within a month of the Big One, most of the network was operational again; the freeway system, by contrast, took years. Public-transit adoption accelerated as a result. By the time the Black Fever Pandemic arrived, Southern California had become a metropolis built around its rail and bus networks, with cars relegated to a secondary role for the first time in the region's history.
The freedom of expression that personal driving had once offered did not vanish without finding new outlets. Demand for pilot sports - racing series where the human still held the controls - surged through the 2030s. Formula 1 became more popular in cities like Neo Los Angeles than it had ever been at its peak in Europe. It was a conduit for the adrenaline and self-determination that auto-driving had taken from the daily commute, and it set the cultural conditions that the Helios Racing League would later exploit.


Disaster Ridge
Unbeknownst to anyone, regions in South California along the San Andreas fault would turn out to be an excellent location to extract a precursor material to Helium-3 Fusion fuel. The tectonic movement of the ground around the San Andreas fault over millennia had created deposits of atomic Lithium-3. Water then came into the ground during the last ice age in California 10-15k years ago. Since then the moving tectonic plates had water-exposed enough lithium deposits from to  to create the perfect environment for abundant special lithium to breed Helium-3 out of. A deposit this large and undisturbed hadn't been discovered before the Big One. 
 A group of Marines that were stationed nearby before the Big One were tasked with assisting the federal and state governments to secure the perimeter and surrounding areas due to its geological interest and proximity to destroyed population centers with massive housing issues. The mass-housing issues caused people to roam the streets for resources in hopes of surviving the disaster while the federal and state government got aid to every one of the hundreds of thousand unhoused, hurt and logistically isolated from resources. The ridge was already local gossip only a couple weeks from its opening and began attracting people looking for shelter. But the unsafe conditions and need to perform geological work and extract the lithium made it untenable for a refugee site. What first started out as Combat Outpost Isotope would grow over time to become Oasis City - the location of Earth Tower 1’s construction. 
Evolution of Disaster Ridge Site
Combat Outpost Isotope, or ​COP Isotope, is the original name of the base after it was established that massive L^6 were present after the initial survey science-personnel only deployment. Lazaro is designated as a scientific liaison for the marines deployed there. He becomes the point of contact for the researchers carrying out the surveying portion of the mission. The designation is austere and entirely focused on the immediate mission of securing and surveying with better certainty the entire deposits in the immediate aftermath of the earthquake.
​FOB Oasis becomes the new designation as extraction operations scale up and the site requires broader logistical support and infrastructure. For the first time there is an elevator built from the command center at the top of the ridge to the bottom of it. It allows the scientists to bring heavier and more sensitive surveying equipment, mining tools, miners and military personnel for the first time. The top and bottom levels of the Ridge are aptly called Top Ridge and Bottom Ridge. Over time this simplified to Top and Bottom. Temporary housing in large military tents are set up on the bottom level. As Lazaro is scientific liaison, he is among the first Marines sent for any work to the bottom of Disaster Ridge. This time the name introduces the irony of the desert environment while reflecting a steady influx of resources, personnel, and early corporate contractors.
Under the name Camp Oasis, the installation shifts from a temporary forward operating base to a permanent, fortified garrison and personnel quarters. Lazaro is part of the first group that receives permanent housing in the newly flattened bottom levels of the Ridge. At this stage, the expanded military and engineering efforts required the support of a growing workforce responsible for breeding the Lithium-6 into Helium-3 on-site. A small town had begun up top with a smaller one below. It was time to incorporate it as a city.
 Oasis City - given to the area after the military designation is dropped entirely as corporate and civilian entities took the region over from the military. Transforming the region into a technical power house and mass-scale industrial mining operation. NovaTech had technically bought the rights to the region from the US government in order to do so.
Star City is the final name change for the region after MEGACORP takes over the region when it is decided to build the space elevator in the Ridge. The completed space elevator transforms the former disaster zone and military perimeter into a centralized aerospace and economic epicenter of astronomical proportion.

NovaTech and the Two Reactors
Lazaro's doctorate, begun at UCLA in the fall of 2047 and completed in the spring of 2052, was sponsored by NovaTech under proprietary terms — the company funding the work and owning whatever came of it. What came of it, on paper, was the p-B¹¹ reactor design: the first aneutronic fusion reactor that could be made small and clean enough to fly, proven out in simulation. He invented the reactor, not the drive — the Direct Fusion Drive and the whole ecology of craft built on it came later, as others productized what he had proven possible (see DFD Vehicle Classes). What he handed NovaTech was the design and the simulations. What he did not hand them — what he never told them existed — was the working unit he had already built by hand in his home workshop, to satisfy himself the thing was real before he would put his name to it. He could not trust a number on a screen he hadn't held in his hands first; it was the same instinct that would later have his younger son taking apart every toy he owned. That hand-built first unit — the true first p-B¹¹ reactor in the world — is the one the brothers would come to call the genesis reactor. NovaTech never knew it existed.
The company kept him on after the doctorate, his work spanning both the giant D-He³ ground reactors rising to power Earth Tower 1 and the development of his own p-B¹¹ design. In 2053 he built the first commercial p-B¹¹ unit with NovaTech — the reactor that flew the first DFD craft — and the company took it, proudly, to be the world's first, never suspecting that one had been quietly running in a garage for over a year. It was this expertise, more than his service record, that won him his seat on Project Athena in 2063 as the Enceladus's nuclear propulsion engineer: the ship would fly on a scaled-up version of the reactor he had created.
He received no public credit for any of it. Under his sponsorship, every patent and drawing was NovaTech's from the moment of conception, and the work was published under the company's name. In the records that mattered, NovaTech invented the p-B¹¹ reactor; Lazaro invented nothing. It is why the world could meet him, in the Athena coverage, as a relatively irrelevant crew member — an engineer among engineers — and never know it was looking at the man who built the drive that carried the mission. The company owned his mind and the design it produced. The one thing it never owned, because it never knew to look for it, was the genesis reactor itself. It stayed in the home workshop on his own land, and because its fuel was the one thing NovaTech could never put a meter on, it would run for anyone who could keep it lit. It was still there when he left for the Enceladus in 2070, and still there after MEGACORP buried him and stripped his family — the secret first machine of the entire fusion-craft age, sitting in a garage. It is the drive at the heart of X's Kart, and after it the Rustbucket. X races, without the world ever knowing it, on the true first reactor of his sport — the one his father hid from the people who owned everything else he ever made.
North Korean War
In the later years of Kim Jung Un’s life, he felt unaccomplished. The promises he had made his people to reunify Korea under his leader seemed to be getting further and further away from him. Upset about the failures of his tenure and lack of notable legacy, Un grew an intense, strong resentment towards the world. In his mind, it was the World's fault, largely the western countries, for his inability to enact North Korean international aims. His resentment gave him the motivation to create a device that would poison the world as a payback for rejecting and undermining him. A Doomsday Device. 
Originally started in 2020, over the years KJU researched, developed and tested many Doomsday Mechanisms as they came to be called to different levels of completion. The two most developed weapon efforts, led by the craziest minds in the military's R&D departments - Ri Hyok-chol & Pak Yong-sik, would leave a stain so dark on history nothing in his legacy would ever outlive this blunder. The first was a conventional nuclear salted Doomsday Bomb meant to make space travel and communication impossible through poisoning the Earth's atmosphere with radioactive material for hundreds of years. The second was a virus that would be extremely lethal, transmissive and as psychologically damaging as possible. With this and many other designs under his belt he amassed a number of safe guards towards the toppling of his regime. He reasoned that should his country ever be attacked - he needed cheaper and uninterceptible weapons of mutually assured mass destruction. He didn't care if he didn't live through it, attacking him would cause him to bring the world down along with him. 
On December 31st, 2041, a morbidly obese and increasingly senile leader of North Korea, Kim Jung Un, in an inebriated state fumbles his way to the war room he had installed next to his bedroom. Accompanied by one of his pleasure girls, he latches himself to her in an attempt to seduce her. In his excited and delirious state he mistakenly hits one of the many big red buttons in the room and launches a single nuclear missile. Painfully aware of this inevitability, the South Korean government had set up automatic conventional anti-missile systems that were always superior to their North Korean counterparts. The defense system swiftly detected the missile and enacted its vast counter measures as the lone ICBM ascended over the Korean peninsula. With its superior missile defense technology, the South Korean defense system was able to neutralize the missile.
Granted that the nuclear missile might explode if hit with a conventional missile strike, the protocol for such an event called for it to be destroyed at peak altitude. The peak altitude disposal they argued would save anyone on the ground from the most immediate risks. Had they known it was a salted bomb however, the optimal strategy wouldn't be to shoot it down at its apogee. Salted bombs should be exploded as close to the ground as possible. If it goes too high the radioactive material, or salt as the metaphor goes, lingers in the planet's atmosphere and exosphere. The salting causes space travel by people and computers alike to be much more dangerous. Disastrously, the effect can remain for days to centuries depending on how salted or radioactive the material is and how high the warhead is detonated.
 As it turned out for the people of Earth - the worst of those scenarios had occurred. The nuclear thermal missile carrying 10 metric tons of heavy, unrefined Uranium-238 material exploded 6000km above Earth. Immediately, the vaporized Tamper, or the salted shell the bomb was encased in, filled the Earth's upper atmosphere with radioactive material rich in Strontium-90 and Cesium-137 of all sizes. This caused all satellite communication in the area to drop as the first and strongest round of plasma and radiation traveled. It instantly fried hundreds of satellites unlucky enough to be nearby. As the cap of radioactive debris spread across the world at 6000km and below, communication with all objects beyond 6000 and dropped started to go out too. 
Immediate Response
The United States government was the first to become aware of the situation due to its vast synthetic radar military constellations. It gave them the opportunity to watch for rocket launches anywhere in the world with milliseconds of latency. Already in communication with the South Korean government before the warhead exploded, they were the first to feel the effects of the dirty bomb. MEO and high LEO satellites in the US military and information agencies were hit first as they were mostly theirs. South Korean and soon everyone's geo-stationary satellites began having performance issues with reliable communication. Satellites below had yet to be affected but were soon going to be bombarded by radiation in the following months and years as well. 
Implications on Space Travel
The military and now NASA, both being aware and working together on the situation, moved to ground every astronaut as soon as possible though they were still in the clear for the arrival of unsafe radiation levels. As the cloud of radiation turned from a ball to a blanket in the hours that followed detonation, other regions of the globe in the under the blankets orbit started to feel the effects as well. All other space agencies - ESA, China, Roscosmos, ISRO & JAXA - quickly followed NASAs lead and began operations to bring their astronauts below the blanket home. These would be the astronauts in LEO stations. Their other grave task was to prepare their satellites for the highly radioactive fallout that's about to rain past them. Radio ground stations could no longer communicate with GEO, L2 and beyond, observational satellites. Missions to the Moon, Mars, and the rest of the Solar System became unreachable. The permanent US and Chinese Moon Bases were completely in the blind. Unsure of what had happened, the Moon Bases both chose to pause operations and prepare an emergency evacuation while they tried to get back in contact with their ground controls. Awareness that the other Moon Base personnel would be the only ones physically capable of helping one another was not lost on either crew. The US and Chinese bases already in contact through international and regulated communication channels broke with convention and communicated directly to each other without overseers or filters. 
The explosion would end up creating a decades-long cascade of nuclear decaying material raining down on Earth making it hazardous for people and computers to travel through. The material would create a new kind of unpredictable space weather that lingered with varying intensity in pockets of every changing space below and slightly above 6000km. Similar to how the wind pushes clouds that accumulate into large storms of rain, solar wind and gravity push and pull the radioactive material into storms of debris and radiation. 


Sour Rain
The salted bomb's first inheritance was the poisoning of orbit. Its second, slower and all but unnoticed at the time, was the poisoning of the rain. The mechanism is a single one wearing two faces. The fallout that filled the sky above 6,000 km did not stay put. The portion that never reached orbital velocity fell back within hours and weeks, scattered across a turning planet; the portion caught in low orbit spiraled down over months and years as its orbits decayed; and all of it, once it reached the upper atmosphere, hung there a year or three as fine suspended particulate before settling lower still. Only the highest core, in true orbit at 6,000 km and above, stayed up - and that fraction will stay up for centuries, the drag too thin to bring it down. This is why the orbital hazard and the rain are the same disaster sorted by altitude: the high material menaces space, the descended material poisons the ground.
What that descended particulate does in the lower air is twofold. Its radiation ionizes the nitrogen and oxygen around it, breaking the molecules apart and recombining them into nitrogen oxides - the same radiolytic nitrogen fixation that lightning performs in a storm, but running continuously, fed by Strontium-90 and Cesium-137 instead of an electric arc. Those oxides dissolve into atmospheric moisture as nitric acid, and the rain turns corrosive. And the very particles doing the ionizing are scavenged into the droplets they help to sour, so the rain comes down not only acidic but faintly radioactive - never enough to be the thing that kills a person outright, but enough to set a counter ticking, to stain the runoff, to leave a residue on every surface it touches. People stopped calling it acid rain, which named only half of it, and called it sour rain instead: rain that had not merely turned acidic but gone bad.
Nothing industrial caused it. Two centuries of unchecked emissions from the nineteenth century onward had left the atmosphere thin-skinned and easily tipped, and that head start is sometimes blamed - but the driver was the fallout and nothing else. It is also why the sour rain outlived every other scourge of the age: it was never burning anything, so the fusion transition that scrubbed combustion out of the world's energy supply did nothing to touch it. The driver was decay, and decay keeps its own schedule.
It arrived as a slope, not a switch. The first faint acidity was in the rain within a year of the detonation, in 2042 - but the world was underground and dying of Black Fever by then, and no one was measuring the pH of a sky they could not safely stand under. The acidity climbed all through the pandemic as the low-orbit fallout decayed in, unattended and unnamed. By the time humanity surfaced into the Reconstruction it was undeniable, and over the years that followed it matured into its full and violent form, established by the early 2050s, roughly a decade after the bomb. Then it began its centuries-long decline, fading on the same clock as the debris overhead. The cruelest part was the order of it: a generation clawed its way out from under the plague only to walk up into a world where the sky itself had turned against them.
And it does not fall evenly. The same solar wind and gravity that herd the orbital debris into drifting storms govern how much particulate is delivered where and when, so the sour rain arrives in discrete, moving fronts rather than a steady drizzle - a clear afternoon can sour to a building-stripping downpour within the hour as a cell drifts overhead, then clear again behind it. The fronts wring out hardest over the metropolitan areas, where the heat of the city drives the convection that lifts the moisture and the city's own haze gives the droplets something to form on. Open desert and ocean see a milder, diffuse fall; it is the cities, with the most to lose, that take the worst of it. At their most concentrated the cells will char exposed skin and kill unprotected vegetation in a single fall, and over years they will eat through anything not built to stand them.
Stone Construction
Nitric acid is merciless to the materials of the old world. Limestone, marble, and ordinary cement-bound concrete are calcium-rich, and calcium reacts with nitric acid to form a soluble nitrate that simply washes away - stone and mortar dissolving from the surface inward, which is why the old cities seem, across decades, to be slowly melting. The same chemistry that brought down Los Angeles's century-old brick and masonry in the Big One finished the work by rain.
What endures is silicate stone, which offers the acid nothing soluble to take. Quartzite, nearly pure silica, is the most inert of all; granite and basalt follow. Through the late 2040s and 2050s, as the sour rain settled into a permanent fact of the climate, terrestrial construction abandoned concrete facing and carbonate stone and turned to quarried granite and basalt for anything meant to last a century. The first-generation Megablocks, poured in concrete before the rain turned, had to be clad in stone retrofit by retrofit through the Reconstruction - a visible seam in the architecture of every old arcology, dividing what was raised before the sky changed from what came after. The finest material of all, though, came down from the Moon (see Cast Mare Regolith).


Black Fever
Creation
At the time of X's birth, everyone knew that the black fever virus was created, perhaps accidentally - perhaps not, by the appalling conditions of the North Korean government's internment camps. It is said that one of North Korea's top doctors was developing a new biological weapon to get back at the western world for what they saw as their humiliating dominance over them. The suspected man of the medical experiments that gave rise to the virus was the North Korean Surgeon General - Ri Hyok-chol.
A life long friend of Kim Jung Un's, he became one of his most fanatical supporters once he rose to power. Even by the standards of the North Korean subjects, the other NK elites saw his praise and adoration as excessive. This level of fanaticism was rewarded by being in Kim Jung Un's closest circles, giving the Ri Hyok-chol access to the supreme leader's ideas over national health and subsequently human medical experimentation. The idea to seek revenge on the world for NKs low global standing as a result of the western power’s meddling was not new. What was new was the Ri's zeal for creating a new biological weapon as opposed to the conventional nuclear arms they had been using to scare western allies for decades. Owing to the fact that NK was a self insulated country, a biological weapon in the form of a virus wouldn't affect them much so Ri explained. It would be the most silent but destructive terrorist attack in world history. He argued that it had to be homegrown but once the virus was isolated it would be carried into the wild world by unsuspecting defectors. This would achieve two things. First, it would make SK the first and most gravely affected country by the time the world caught onto what was going on. Secondly it would be spread only on the backs of NK citizens that chose to desert their country and dear leader - the most unforgivable of betrayal in NK society - and therefore, in their eyes, was completely just. The key to creating the most impactful and effective biological weapon thought Ri Hyok-chol was not a hyper deadly virus that killed as many people as possible. Rather he aimed his sights on creating the most dramatic and emotionally scarring infirmity possible. Something whose effects were so visceral and palpable there wouldn't be a single person that felt safe. Thus the desired effect was chosen to be some kind of externally extruding ailment. 
Being NK the general has no shortage of test subjects. Political enemies, old friends that knew too much about their dear leader, homosexuals, the disabled, etc. were all available for experimentation. None of these groups however would be as satisfying to perform their malice on than attempted emigres and western foreigners. Adventuristic journalists, POWs from failed assassination plots and misguided influencers would make up the majority of the first test subjects. They after all wanted it to work best on these kinds of people. At first their work was a big guessing game. Ri Hyok-chol had to infect people with common diseases and expose test subjects to each other while they suffered the worst of their illnesses. After a couple years of disease incubation and development he found the virus he had been looking for. It exhibited symptoms similar to the bubonic plague by creating buboes or sacks of deoxygenated blood and pus. The virus would latch itself to a victim's singular body part instead of developing all over the body. The virus seemed to enjoy setting up its temporary home in areas of high blood oxygenation. That would include the lungs, heart and large muscles such as the thighs, quadriceps and gluteus. With these results in mind, the Ri wanted to focus on exploding the heart by coaxing the virus to latch to the heart instead of any other part the majority of the time. What he found instead was much more visually disturbing. 
One of his subjects began showing symptoms abnormal to the others. Instead of internal or external buboes developing, no abnormal parts of the body began to grow. Rather the doctors were only able to detect a mild but persistent fever. It wasn't until one of the nurses, on one of her routine morning checks on the subjects, found the abnormal presenting subject lying in an almost unnatural amount of blood. The scene resembled more of a shotgun suicide than anything else. All that was missing from the body was the subject's head. Pieces of skull and brain matter dressed the walls like wallpaper. When the nurse reported her findings to the doctors on staff she was met with incredulity. Afraid that they might lose everything for losing a subject so bizarrely, before they even checked the cameras and investigated the matter, the head on-site doctor called the military police and had her arrested for assisting in the subject’s “escape” and potentially derailing the experiments. They did so in such a hurry to avoid suspicion of helping the nurse in whatever she might or might not have done. Ri, being alerted of the situation, rushed to the lab. By the time he arrived, the doctors and head of security were huddled over a computer monitor. Shoving and shouting his way through the crowd of his peers, he paused once he finally saw what they were fixated on. They could see the subject laying asleep in their bed. The fever she displayed signs of had gotten visibly worse. She was struggling in her sleep, moaning and groaning with streaks of sweat beading down her face. Just as the worst seemed to pass, she started to scream; sitting up in a violent jolt she met her hands with the side of her head when, in an instant, it burst into mist. The screaming stopped as quickly as it began and her headless corpse slammed back onto the bed with a bang. Back in the control room, the men watching the monitor took a second to compose themselves. They began asking themselves and each other what they had just seen. The conversations ceased when the Ri Hyok-chol broke everyone’s concentration with a deep, guttural laugh. Sounding almost maniacal and putting his comrades in a state of unease, he was the only to realize that the reactions his peers displayed was exactly what they had been looking for. He congratulated his team, most still in shock and reluctant to feel any glee or sense of accomplishment. Once his giddiness subsided and the moment passed, he scanned the room. Noticing his favorite morning nurse gone, he asked the head morning doctors for her status. Now realizing that sending her to the clutches and machinations of the secret police was a mistake, he ashamedly explained in low voice what he had done. The expression of joy that lingered from the success of their experiments quickly vanished. The head morning doctors on paper hadn’t done anything wrong. It was protocol, when things go wrong, to find someone to blame and get rid of them by sending them to the secret police. If it had been anyone else he wouldn’t have cared. However unbeknownst to anyone else in the room he had the nurse had been secretly seeing each other. These clandestine rendezvous had evolved into sexual encounters. It had only been a few days that Ri had known he was going to be a father. The on-site doctors had unknowingly sentenced Ri’s lover and their unborn child to death. 
Initial Spread
When the Black Fever Virus reached the wider world, two transmission vectors operated simultaneously - and only one of them was politically convenient to discuss. The dominant vector was military: the armies of every nation that invaded North Korea, exposed to the camps and to one another during the chaos of the salted-bomb aftermath, carried the BFV home in their bodies. The secondary vector was the climate-refugee flow already underway through the CCADIF-era open-borders regime. Refugees became the public-facing scapegoat for the virus's spread - millions of people on the move, easy to point at - while the real first-mover responsibility lay with the militaries of the same governments doing the pointing.
Had the BFV emerged at the start of the climate-refugee era, before any awareness of it existed, the disease would likely have ended human civilization. As it happened, the virus surfaced first in returning soldiers, and the world had a brief window to recognize it. Brief but not enough: by the time the President was informed on February 22nd, 2042, the BFV had already crossed every border the United States military had crossed coming home.


Symptom Description
Black Fever is a condition where a virus crosses the blood brain barrier and lives within the capillary system of the brain. It starts by choosing a location to nest and stays dormant for a couple weeks. In this time, the virus multiplies and spreads to other parts of the brain where they begin the same process over again and their countdown to estimated rupture starts. While there, it sustains itself off the hemoglobin in the blood, taking oxygen from healthy blood cells and letting them asphyxiate. It then uses the carcasses of the cells, along with surrounding neural tissue and brain-blood proteins, to build up a localized structure called a Morticell. The Morticell catches more healthy cells until pressure builds up too much and the surrounding capillary clots or pops, releasing the excess of live and dead blood cells. In other words they give patients a massive stroke. As the virus spreads and establishes additional Morticells across the brain, these individual sites link into a single coordinated structure called the Morticell Net. The net hijacks the host's neural pathways to regulate the growth rate of each Morticell and synchronize the moment of rupture across every site at once. A slight to moderate fever is the only symptom perceived by the affected until rupture.  


Notes:
Black fever is not a very well thought out idea yet but the point is for the world to go into a crisis from the emergence of a new, nearly imperceptible disease. The disease kills millions upon millions, a large percentage of the human population, and destabilizes even the most prosperous and established nations. Existing public and academic institutions weaken while private companies are able to sell and profit off of their new technologies developed for disease and diseased persons tracking, brain scanning and other related tech. Only the most prosperous nations can afford them and as such the middle and poorer classes of the world suffer the most. These developments allow a new level of persons tracking never seen before in history. 
Concurrently, labs are set up almost overnight around the world by the leading and somewhat insulated biomedical corporations to understand but ultimately get the most profit out of the eradication of the Black Fever virus. They do so by first learning how to grow all kinds of brain tissue from individual cell types in a controlled and systematic way. Scientists around the world have a communications network set up to connect the leading labs’ ideas and discoveries. One of their first achievements is the advances in miniaturization of MRI and brain scanning tech. They are able to produce devices that can scan individuals brains for the BF virus individually and cost-effectively en masse by detecting the formation of Morticells and the Morticell Net linking them. Another one of their breakthroughs is the creation of synthetic antibodies. They isolate the virus and are able to train bio-engineered samples of antibodies to attack the virus by running massive parallel test trials. The trials consist of racks upon racks of petri dishes with synthetic brain cells of all types, each testing a different version of the antibodies. The purpose of the trials are to find the most effective antibodies but also the ones with the most brain permeability. The human brain is the most complex organ in the human body and as such also has the most diverse types of cells. This implies that not all antibodies will have the same efficacy for killing the virus in all parts of the brain. In these tests they grow hundreds of thousands of synthetically grown brain tissue. Because they need to check each part of the brain, they get acquainted with the eccentricities of each brain tissue type and their general properties. This includes the speed of information travel through different tissues but also the way they grow. The sheer amount of monitoring and information generated by the tests quickly oversaturates the existing computational network capabilities. In a stroke of genius a sub-group of the Italian contingency tasked with solving this issue computationally combined a number of cell types with the fastest information throughput and created a bio-synthetic signals/information processing unit designed specifically for collecting and analyzing the test data. 
Aftermath
Black Fever didn’t just kill hundreds of millions of people but marked the lives of those that survived for the rest of their life. How does one process not just the death of their family and friends, but watch their most human and identifiable features, their face and head, explode into oblivion. Many people, as X would in the future, developed PTSD from the trauma associated with seeing their loved ones die so gruesomely. The world at large was traumatized as a result of the gruesomeness and isolation caused by the Black Fever. As the reconstruction era progressed and the Black Fever era came to a close, the executives of the private bio mega corporations anticipated a dwindling of their profits since the largest of their products had become the personnel tracking, Black Fever testing and other Black Fever related services. Very quickly it became apparent that society, the ones that survived, had not done so unscathed. A lot of people were entirely different, hollow husks of their previous selves. Suicides had become a popular alternative for people wanting to snuff the virus that had infected them. Even after the development of the cure however the number of suicides did not go down as much as sociologists had predicted. Seeing this as their next opportunity for profit driven benevolence, a flurry of new research was kicked off, as Black Fever research was wound down. The result was a new kind of antipsychotic medication that could treat schizophrenic hallucinations, post-traumatic trances and phantom pain as well as a series of other psychosomatic and psychological infirmities. The medication came in the form of eye drops to be taken at least once per day. They would become known as Neuro-Optic Stabilizer Solutions or NOSSs.


NSPD-51A
Origin/Creation
        The plan, designated as National Security Presidential Directive 51-Alpha (NSPD-51A), an edited version of NSPD-51 originally intended to safeguard the continuity of the US government, is changed by the president after realizing that scientists not politicians would be the key ingredient to defeating the BF virus. 
Structure
A new form of simplified government would be created underground where a subgroup of the scientists, chosen by their peers, would act as a new Congress intended to replace the old one while they're underground. It would take a form similar to that of the old house of representatives where 435 representatives voted in a parliamentary system. No Senate or Supreme Court, meant that this group and the president alone would have all decision making powers in an effort to speed up decision making and do so by the best of the best the scientific world had to offer.
United Earth
Formation from the Underground
During the Black Fever era, as governments of both small and independent nations began to weaken and collapse, essential personnel retreated to highly secure, disaster-proofed underground facilities to insulate themselves from the virus. This isolation necessitated a simplified and highly efficient governance structure. Early iterations of this could be seen in the United States' adaptation of NSPD-51A, which replaced the traditional Congress with a select group of leading scientists to prioritize rapid, expert-driven decision-making. As international cooperation became the only viable path to survival and vaccine distribution, these isolated national remnants merged their communications and operations. This unified network eventually formalized into a singular world government, acting as the definitive, centralized successor to the United Nations named - United Earth.
First Steps
The first meeting. held virtually, was held on May 1st, 2042. Every country was given the opportunity to speak with the opportunity to explain: their situation, their problems, their needs and how they could help. When it was the United States’ turn the President elaborated on NPSD-51A. He explained how the US had already started Project Xero on its own. Every nation in the UEA was invented to help participate. Even through virtual discussion, the excitement for the USs proposal and general direction was palpable. The world had been attacked by the BFV for a few months now and things were starting to get out of control. The President then promptly proposed that the rest of the UEA nations were welcomed to assist the project and be part of the first to reap the rewards. Between a mix of Unity and Pride, representatives from all countries were in clear agreement that they would all cooperate to eradiate the virus. Thus the global Project Xero was born.
Project Xero
Being tasked with saving mankind is no small feat. Per the US President’s example, all other nations bolstered their military R&D departments and brought in the top civilian researchers in their country to their Underground Labs Projects. From there infrastructure was set in place for each of these labs and their governments to have unprecedentedly open communication and logistics channels. The Xero Network became its name. The backbone of the entire project.

The most influential party in the UE, when the President made the suggestion on the Assembly floor, the resolution for every UEA member to do the same and support Project Xero passed unanimously.

Cure Development
Once the underground labs had power and shelter, they had to confront the work itself. The strategy adopted by the Xero Network was brute-force parallelism - given an unknown virus and no time, run every plausible counter-attack at once and see which ones survived contact with the disease.
The first wave went into substrate. Before any cure could be tested, the labs had to learn how to grow human brain tissue from individual cell types in a controlled, repeatable way. The brain is the most complex organ in the human body, with the widest diversity of cell types, and the labs needed to test antibody candidates against every part of it - a virus that could be killed in the cortex but not in the brainstem was useless. Within eighteen months, the network had developed cultivation protocols for over a hundred distinct neural tissue varieties.
The second wave went into screening. Detecting whether a Morticell had formed in a sample required imaging, and pre-pandemic MRI machines were too large, too slow, and too few. A miniaturization program led primarily by Austrian and German imaging specialists at the Bundeswehr Eifel network produced compact scanners small enough to inspect individual petri dishes. The same scanner architecture, scaled and ruggedized, would later become the global Black Fever screening device that every survivor learned to fear - a piece of pandemic infrastructure that pivoted, almost without modification, into a worldwide person-tracking system once the virus was beaten.
The third wave was the antibody trials themselves. Racks of patterned synthetic brain tissue were inoculated with the Black Fever Virus and then exposed to bio-engineered antibody candidates, each candidate slightly different from the last. The labs were not searching for a single cure; they were searching for the antibody with the best brain permeability - one that could reach Morticells in every tissue type and kill the virus before it linked into a full Morticell Net. The trials ran in parallel across every UEA member's underground lab simultaneously, with the Pacific Missile Range Facility in Kauai serving as the network's central coordination point.
The data load was unsurvivable. By late 2043 the antibody trials were producing more monitoring data per day than the entire Project Xero compute infrastructure could ingest. It was this exact bottleneck that forced the Italian sub-group at Gran Sasso to invent the neucomp in early 2044 - a problem-solution event whose secondary effect, the birth of bio-computing, would reshape the post-pandemic world more deeply than the cure it was built to find.
The cure itself arrived on March 29th, 2045, through the Kauai team's neuromorphic synthesis. By that point, the neucomp architecture had been operating for over a year.


Perimeter Control and Base Attrition
The surface military bases were, for a time, the only functioning institutions left in the open world - and that made them magnets for the desperate. Newly single parents who had lost everything but their children came to the quarantined perimeters pleading for entry, swearing the child was immune even as the virus was visibly killing the parent. The planners of Project Xero refused to risk the perimeter garrisons, the network's only surface link. Troops were ordered to turn families away, and when despair overruled sense and they would not leave, to fire on them. At installations like Camp Oasis this happened three to five times a week. Over months the dirt before the gates turned a muted, iron red that stopped abruptly at the treeline of the approach - the only marker of what the ground had absorbed.
Not every base held. Some fell to infiltration when personnel slipped the perimeter and brought the virus back in. Others fell to themselves: factional splits between those who wanted to abandon Project Xero and run an insulated city of their own - defection, in effect - and those who tried to stop them, escalating into attempted coups and internal fighting. Between 2041 and 2046 the surface was a patchwork of disease and mutiny. The only place on Earth genuinely insulated from both was underground, in the labs of the UEA.
Global Corporatization 
The Knowledge Collapse and Forced Monopolization
The Black Fever pandemic eradicated hundreds of millions of people worldwide, severely impacting densely populated urban centers due to the higher transmission rates. Consequently, an immense volume of first-hand human capital was lost. This loss was most catastrophic in highly specialized, artisanal, and bleeding-edge technological sectors — such as advanced silicon processor manufacturing — where institutional knowledge bordered on a blend of art and science.
Silicon was the lesson no one forgot. Its supply chain was the most fragmented and globe-spanning of any industry on Earth, split among dozens of fiercely competitive firms that each held one irreplaceable fragment of the whole and shared none of it. When the pandemic broke those chains, no surviving entity held enough of the process to restart it, and the most advanced manufacturing humanity had ever achieved simply died — not for lack of demand, but because competition had scattered the knowledge too thin to survive a shock. It was the cautionary tale the rest of the policy answered to, and the gap the neucomp would later be grown to fill.
The United Earth treated the pandemic as what it was: a war for the survival of the species, and like every total war before it, one no economy could fight while its industries fought each other. Competition was recast as a luxury of peacetime — the engine of progress when humanity had time to spare, a fatal redundancy when it did not. Realizing that fractured competition would lead to the permanent loss of these irreproducible skills, the United Earth and surviving national entities intervened. Traditional antitrust and competition laws were universally suspended. Corporations were mandated to merge and pool their remaining intellectual and material resources.
In practice the law trailed behind a fusion that had already begun on its own. The specialists who would later staff the megacorporations had been forced into open communication and shared logistics through the Xero Network for years, and under that pressure the walls between former rivals quietly dissolved long before any statute recognized it. The mandate did not so much create the megacorporations as draw borders around mergers that had already happened in the field.
What began as an emergency measure never unwound. Every prior war economy had demobilized once the fighting stopped, but this one had nothing to demobilize back into: the pandemic had killed so deeply that un-merging an entity meant splitting its last surviving specialists below the threshold at which their knowledge could stay alive at all. And the emergency never truly ended — it rolled directly from curing the plague into rebuilding the world, the same continental-scale coordination redirected toward fusion, Earth Tower 1, and the lunar economy, with no moment at which the old competitive order could plausibly have returned. The wartime architecture calcified into the permanent shape of the global economy.
History would record the consolidation as an act of collective sacrifice, humanity pooling its commercial might to save itself. That account is true. It is simply not the whole of it: the same measure that saved the species also enclosed the entirety of human industry into a handful of hands, and the people into whose hands it fell took care, as the mergers were drawn, to secure their own permanence within them (see Project Afterlife). Both things happened at once, and the world that resulted cannot be understood by believing only one.


The Tiered Corporate Structure
This era of aggressive consolidation resulted in a highly structured, tiered global economy. Business horizontals were strictly divided among a designated number of mega-corporations based on their host nation's pre-collapse economic standing. 
The assignment was not arbitrary, and it was largely decided underground. During Project Xero, the national contingency that solved a given technical problem in its labs became the natural home of the horizontal built on it afterward — the wartime division of scientific labor hardening into the peacetime industrial map. A nation's GDP tier then set how many such horizontals it could anchor: less a reward for wealth than a measure of how deep a surviving talent bench a country had to stake an entire industry on, since each horizontal was by design a global single point of failure that someone had to be capable of holding alone.


* Tier 1 Economies (Top 10 GDP Nations): Ultra-wealthy nations, such as the United States, China and Germany were permitted a maximum of a dozen mega-corporations. Each conglomerate was granted absolute monopoly over specific technological or industrial sectors to maximize efficiency. 
* Tier 2 Economies (Rank 10-50 GDP Nations): Medium-sized economies were heavily consolidated, leaving only two to five mega-corporations per nation. These entities managed broader manufacturing and service portfolios necessary for regional stability and specialized international trade, such as the Indonesian conglomerate's focus on 3D-printed organs or the Mexican conglomerate's monopoly on synthetic skins.
* Tier 3 Economies (Sub-50 GDP Nations): Smaller economies, such as Bulgaria, Ukraine, or Sri Lanka, were entirely consolidated into a single national mega-corporation. These unitary corporate entities focused exclusively on the region's most historically successful or resource-rich exports, acting as highly specialized nodes within the global supply chain orchestrated by the United Earth.


The Disclosure Mandate
The price of outlawing competition was visibility. In exchange for sanctioned monopoly, every Megacorp is legally bound to disclose its major developments and product releases to the public - the social contract that makes planetary-scale monopoly tolerable to the people living under it. Without it, the Megacorp system would be experienced as pure extraction with no return, and the public would tear it down. No actor on Earth, however wealthy, is powerful enough to repeal it; the mandate sits above any single corporation or fortune. Its unintended consequence is the engine of the LIFEstream conflict: because every advance in space technology must be shown to the world, the dream of human spaceflight keeps reigniting in the public imagination no matter how many times the powerful try to extinguish it. Atticus can buy ventures, engineer deaths, and stage spectacles - but he cannot switch off the disclosures that keep the dream alive against him.
The Biomedical Surge
The collapse of the academic and public-institutional sector during the pandemic created a parallel vacuum the Knowledge Collapse process did not anticipate. Universities, hospitals, and government health agencies had absorbed the worst per-capita losses of any sector - they were where the sick went, and where the sick were studied - and the few institutions that retained core staff did so in deep isolation. Meanwhile the biomedical megacorporations of the pre-pandemic era, insulated by their ability to lock down private campuses early and screen their own personnel, came out of the BF era with the most intact specialist talent base on Earth.
This was not an accident of timing. As the pandemic accelerated, governments turned to those same private firms to manufacture the screening hardware, brain-scanning devices, and person-tracking systems that became the daily texture of pandemic survival. The corporations licensed their equipment to whichever nations could pay. Wealthy nations afforded continuous mass-screening of their populations and built out the surveillance infrastructure that would outlive the pandemic by decades; middle-income and poorer nations rationed their tests, relied on visual diagnosis, and lost a higher fraction of their citizens. By 2045 the biomedical sector had achieved something pre-pandemic regulators would have considered impossible: it had become the sole point of contact between governments and their own populations.
When the Knowledge Collapse mergers were imposed, the biomedical megacorporations were not consolidating from a position of weakness. They were dictating terms. The screening, scanning, and tracking apparatus they had built during the pandemic became the operational foundation of every megacorporation horizontal that followed.
Business Structures
The business structures of the lifestream world are different and less diverse than those of the present day. The need to make businesses and the work they do as efficient and cost effective as possible by horizontal monopolization of entire industries by nation-sized corporations creates these new business dynamics. 
Horizontal Megacorporations
This term describes the baseline entities established during the Black Fever era. They possess a sanctioned, global monopoly over a single stratum of the supply chain or industrial process. Their existence is justified by maximum global efficiency and resource management. They are designed to be interdependent, relying on other Horizontal Megacorporations to function. This interdependence is also what keeps the system honest: no H-Mega can push its monopoly too far, because each is hostage to the others' output, and a horizontal that strangles its partners strangles itself. It is the structural check that makes planetary-scale monopoly survivable rather than purely extractive — and its deliberate violation is exactly what defines the Monolithic megacorp. They are colloquially known as H-Megas or simply Megas. 
Syndicated Megacorporations
This structure emerges when multiple Horizontal Megacorporations form strategic alliances. By stacking their respective horizontal specializations, they create a vertically integrated supply chain through partnership. This allows them to produce finished products collectively. While this increases their combined profit margins and reduces their reliance on outside entities, it maintains the original horizontal boundaries of the individual corporations within the syndicate.
S-Megas are not Megacorporations in the typical sense. They are a decision making vehicle to help multiple H-Megas make decisions together. They also help in brand-recognition since the finalized products are sold by the S-Mega and profits are distributed by said S-Mega entity to the H-Megas. 
Monolithic Megacorporations
This classification applies to entities that actively violate the Black Fever era heuristics. A Monolithic Megacorporation attempts to achieve total market control by encroaching on horizontals outside its original mandate, while simultaneously expanding vertically. This aggressive expansion threatens the established global order and directly causes friction with other corporations and national governments, as it dismantles the cooperative efficiency the system was built upon in search of higher profits. Sometimes called M-Megas.

Corporate Nationalism
The mergers solved the problem of pooled resources but created a quieter one: what was a "Germania" or a "Nordia" now supposed to be? Fusing several nations dissolved several histories, several languages' worth of pride, several sets of heroes, and left a vacuum where national identity used to sit. The horizontal filled it. Lacking an older story they could all share, the merged peoples took their cohesion from the one thing their new nation did better than anyone alive, and over a single generation the enterprise became the identity. To be of Germania was to be one of the people who built the impossible; to be Korean was to be the people who put a human life onto the wire; to be Mexican was to clothe the world in skin it could not tell from its own. The flag and the brand became the same object.
This was less a break with the past than the perfection of an old habit. The pre-collapse world had always pinned a piece of its pride to its finest products — one nation known for the device in every pocket, another for the marque on the impossible car and the name stitched into the dress, a third for the lens-maker without whom no one else's machines could be built at all. But that pride had been partial, one boast among a hundred: a country was famous for its cars and also fed itself, told its own stories, made its own films, argued its own politics. The mergers stripped the hundred down to the one. A nation's proudest export became its only export, and then its only self. A people no longer just led a field - they were the field - the only people on the planet that did that.. National pride and product loyalty fused into a single feeling, and the Helios Racing League is its purest theater: each driver a national champion and a corporate mascot in one suit, with no seam left between them.
For most of the world this was simply literal. The GDP to anchor even one global horizontal was more than most nations could muster, so the great majority came out of the mergers as a single people behind a single product, with nothing else to their name — the purest form of the fusion, because there was nothing else for the identity to compete with. The handful of Tier 1 giants that carried several horizontals at once were the exception, and even they bent toward a single face: one flagship mega, keeper of the nation's most vital horizontal, that the world pictured when it pictured the country. The United States ran six, but it was NovaTech — keeper of the fusion flame — that stood for America; Germania was the impossible things Deutsch Con built, whatever else its smaller megas made. Multiplicity, where it existed at all, still resolved to one name. 
The generation born after the mergers, X's among them, knew no other arrangement; a country being a single product struck them as natural as weather. The older generation could still remember when a Germania had been a Germany and an Austria, when a people had been a thick, contradictory, unprofitable tangle of more than one thing. Something was gained in the trade and something thinned out of the world. The fusion also gave the old corporate rivalries a far more dangerous charge: when a Monolithic megacorp pushes into a horizontal that is not its own, it is no longer felt as mere business but as one nation reaching into the soul of another — which is why such incursions provoke a hostility closer to war than to competition.
This is what makes a nation, in the new order, as disposable as any worker on a MEGACORP roster. A country that is its horizontal and nothing more has staked its whole reason to exist on one industry's continued relevance; let that horizontal be obsoleted, or breached by a Monolithic rival reaching across the line, and what is lost is not market share but the nation itself — its income, its pride, and its identity in a single stroke. The pride is real. It is also a cage with no other room in it.

Earth Tower 1
Conceptualization and Considerations
After the success of the global vaccination campaign to combat the spread of Black Fever the world had at its disposal powerful new technologies and a burning urge to rebuild. No project exemplified this spirit more than the construction of Earth Tower 1. A project of unheard of proportions, it would be the single most expensive construction project in human history. The first and only Terra-scale project. Its success could put humanity on track to innumerable riches from stellar resources. Its failure would bring the world into a recession like no other, impoverishing the world's population to itself for generations to come. 
Earth Tower 1 isn't a space elevator in the traditional sense of the term. The concept is mostly thought of as a tether held under tension by having a counter weight in Geostationary Orbit some 36k-km above the earth equator with a massive counter weight at its end. ET1 on the other hand is a combination of a space reaching tower known as a space fountain with a tether connected to the end at GEO. There are therefore two sections: the Space Fountain and the Space Tether. 
A space fountain is defined as an active-support structure contrary to a passive-support structure of the space tether design. It uses electricity to power massive arrays of magnets to accelerate ferrous material to speeds in excess of 15km/s. The stream of material travels in a vacuum tube to reduce drag and is kept from the tube walls with magnets along the tube to eliminate friction. The kinetic energy possessed by the stream of magnetic material acts as an impetus towards movement in any direction giving the fountain rigidity and stability that the tether design lacks. At the top of the tower, there needs to be a large armored structure, referred to as an anchor, that redirects the material back down to earth. Again, using magnets, the anchor redirects the material along the same vacuum tube it came up on. The entire system is reliant on a constant source of immense levels of power. If the power were ever to stop being delivered, the tower would collapse due to atmospheric and Coriolis forces imparted on it by Death Valley’s 36° tilt relative to the earth's equator. Thus power generation is the largest constraint to building a space fountain. Rough estimates put the power levels required to sustain a 36,000 km pure space fountain at 300–400 Terawatts.. The final architecture resolves the constraints of both designs by splitting their responsibilities. The tether, held under tension by the Atlas counterweight at GEO, bears the primary vertical load - its centrifugal pull supports the bulk of the tower and Port Charon's weight. The fountain provides what the tether cannot: lateral rigidity. Without an active inertial spine, a 2,000 km column hanging from a 36,000 km tether would sway and oscillate destructively from Coriolis forces, atmospheric drag at altitude, payload accelerations, and harmonic resonances. The fountain's high-velocity mass stream resists those perturbations through sheer momentum. Neither subsystem could do the job alone. The hybrid was Deutsch Con's contribution during the 2052–2054 engineering review, and it was what made the whole project economically viable. The hybrid was also the only architecture that could absorb Port Charon's projected growth. By the early 2060s the project's planners were already modeling a logistics city at the top of the fountain - a transshipment point where lunar cargo arriving on the regolith pipeline could be processed before descent. The projected mass curve was steep: a 5-million-tonne hub at opening was the conservative baseline, with long-term forecasts assuming an order of magnitude larger within twenty years. A fountain-only design could never have carried that. The tether's ability to scale its load capacity through counterweight augmentation - slowly, expensively, but indefinitely - was what made Port Charon possible as a concept at all. With total global power consumption levels for the early 2020s at 30TWh, a single geostationary space fountain would require at least 10x times the world's power consumption. Even with Direct Energy Conversion or DEC Fusion reactors producing 100GW each, it would take hundreds of individual reactors to power such a monstrosity. Therefore with the space fountain design the main constraint is how much power one can generate. 
A space tether as mentioned above is a passive-support structure. It uses the centrifugal pull of a counterweight orbiting at geostationary orbit to create tension and pull the tether up. This design is limited by three things: the location of the terrestrial anchor, the weight of the counterweight and the tensile strength of material used for the tether. Due to the destabilizing forces mentioned in the previous paragraph, a space tether must be placed right on the equator. This severely limits the places one can place a space tether and a story with it in America would restrict it to being in Central America which is not where the story takes place. The weight of the counterweight must be put into consideration because how does one place that much mass in orbit to begin with? Existing theoretical designs include lunar regolith and asteroids as candidates. Asteroids are cool but extremely unfeasible due to the amount of fuel required to move one let alone put it in geostationary orbit. Lunar regolith is probably the best choice due to its relative proximity to GEO and its abundance. The most important factor in a tether design however is the material used to keep tension. All current day designs for space tether immediately fall on their face when this topic is brought up. To date no material has the tensile strength needed to support the weight of a 36,000km long tether and its counterweight. A major breakthrough in material science and manufacturing would have to transgress for the tether concept to become plausible reality. 
The building location of the tower is a whole other problem with space elevators of every design. Typically they can only be constructed over the earth’s equator to eliminate Coriolis effect forces. The tower portion is strong enough to resist these torsion forces as it was intended with this in mind. After 2000km the effects are minimal enough for the tether design to withstand the Coriolis and other atmospheric effects. Which is partially why the design is a hybrid. Strong and sturdy tower until it's no longer needed and a cheaper, less involved structure can take it's place to send things up and take some load off the magnetic inertial forces holding up the tower.

Each major expansion at Port Charon - typically driven by spikes in lunar regolith throughput or the addition of new processing infrastructure - has been preceded by months of additional Processed Regolith shipments accreted onto Atlas, raising the tether tension available at the 2,000 km junction. The program has never quite kept pace. By 2084 the entire structure is operating closer to its design margins than its operators publicly acknowledge. This is the operational subtext beneath the Charon's Gauntlet race: the venue is dangerous not just because of the freight traffic in the racing corridors but because the venue itself is a slow-motion engineering crisis, and the racing league is happening on top of it.
The next large hurdle was the fusion reactors on the ground, both to power the tower section and to support the logistics for further construction and eventual space-material sorting and transport. The reactors would be the largest ever built up to that point, of NovaTech design and manufacture, each capable of 1 TW output. The original 2051 design had called for over 300 reactors at 250 GW each to support a pure space fountain configuration, but once Deutsch Con's hybrid tether-fountain architecture was adopted in 2054, the power budget dropped enough to justify fewer, larger units. The final 120-reactor configuration delivered 120 TW total installed capacity. At a time when total world electricity generation was around 100–110 TW, with fusion still only 20% of that, ET1 alone represented an installed capacity equal to global generation. The Star City reactor ring became the most concentrated industrial power source in human history.


Lunar Economy
Overnight, when the announcement of sustainable fusion reactors was made to the public, Helium-3, the isotope needed for the highest energy fusion reactions, became the most expensive and sought after material in the world and beyond. On earth it is a rare material. All naturally occurring Helium-3 decayed long ago when the Earth was still forming. On the moon however the story was entirely different. Because the sun is at the age where it is abundant in Helium-4, and uses it as its primary fuel, the moon is constantly being coated in the radioactive isotope Helium-3. Though because of its radioactivity it has a half-life of a couple days, it decays rapidly, the sun produces a constant amount to deposit on the moon's surface meaning it is a practically unlimited resource. It was clear to all leaders of state and scientists that that single material would shape the course of humanity's future. While the United States and China had both set up lunar bases prior to the advent of the Black Fever, they had since been abandoned. This meant that the United States and China, as well as Russia, were among the only nations after the eradication of Black Fever to have a claim to lunar territory. 
Cast Mare Regolith
The dark basaltic plains of the lunar maria are rich in ilmenite, an iron-titanium oxide — and ilmenite is also the mineral that best traps the Helium-3 implanted by the solar wind. The same rock is valuable twice. To extract Helium-3, the lunar operations heat enormous tonnages of mare regolith to drive off the solar-wind volatiles; what remains is a degassed, titanium-rich basaltic spoil that, sintered or cast, sets into a dense, near-black, marble-veined stone. Workers called it lunar marble for the way it looked, and the name held even though it is the chemical opposite of the carbonate marble it resembles. In the trade it is Cast Mare Regolith, or CMR.
Its resistance to acid comes from three things at once. It is entirely silicate and oxide, with no carbonate for the acid to attack. Its high ilmenite content leaves a titanium-dioxide-stabilized surface, and titanium dioxide is essentially inert to nitric acid. And because it is consolidated in vacuum, with no trapped air or water, it cures to a near-zero-porosity glass-and-crystallite matrix that gives the acid no microchannels to enter — weathering stops at a thin, self-passivating skin rather than working into the body of the stone. No terrestrial material, not even quartzite, matches it. It is cheap in the way only a byproduct can be: no chemical refinement, only the simplest thermal forming, drawn from the spoil heap of an operation already running for something else.
The Backbone of the Tower's Economy
Helium-3 is the cargo that makes the headlines and the fortunes, the most valuable substance on Earth by mass. But it is almost nothing by tonnage; a year's global supply would barely fill a few carriages. The freight that actually fills Earth Tower 1, day in and day out, is CMR. With acid rain making silicate stone the only durable way to build and the Moon the only place to source it at planetary scale, demand is effectively bottomless, every seawall, arcology, and public megastructure on Earth is built or clad in lunar marble. By bulk mass and volume it is the single largest thing moving through the elevator, by a margin nothing else approaches.
This is what closes the economics of the tower. Helium-3 alone could never have justified a structure of ET1's throughput; its mass is small enough to ride conventional rockets. It is the bulk stone trade, the millions of tonnes of CMR that only an elevator can bring down cheaply, that pays the freight and earns the structure its keep. ET1 is as much a stone pipeline as an energy one.


Civilian Air & Space Travel
Pre-Pandemic Era
Beginning in 2028, the American corporation SpaceV emerged as the world's largest launcher of personnel and cargo into space. Their first major civilian product was a point-to-point rocket delivery system. Cargo flight rating required less time and capital than human-rated hardware, so the company moved heavy industrial equipment, climate-relief supplies, and government cargo first - anywhere in the world, in under an hour. Human point-to-point lagged behind. Acceleration loads and turbulence kept the early customer base small: business executives, emergency personnel, and government officials. The SpaceV Starship personnel variant for terrestrial routes did not release until 2032.
For everyone else, supersonic airliners did the work. Built on mature jet-engine technology refined over decades, they began carrying large numbers of passengers in 2027. By 2031 the average commercial flight speed was Mach 2.2. This represented the peak of jet-engine commercial aviation. Like the propeller before it, the jet was on the verge of being superseded - though no one in the industry knew yet by what.
Post-Pandemic Era
Aircraft development resumed in 2045, when distribution planning for the Black Fever cure became the next operational priority of Project Xero. The industry's surviving capacity had been frozen for nearly five years. When it restarted, the legacy supersonic-jet airframes were already dead technology - the Direct Fusion Drive had been quietly maturing inside the underground labs the whole time, and it was ready for commercial adaptation.
By 2048, with global trade picking up, airlines drew up plans to convert their remaining jet fleets to DFD-powered craft - then quickly raised more capital and committed to entirely new clean-sheet designs. The compressed timelines were a side effect of the Megacorp consolidation: with the entire civilian aviation industry now concentrated into AirBus and a handful of partner megacorps, decisions that would have taken a decade pre-pandemic took two years. The first commercial DFD airliner entered service in 2051. By that point no aircraft of any kind had flown in five years. The world picked up where it left off, only faster.


Lifestream Technology
The consumer entertainment sector too was affected by the Cambrian explosion of technologies following the Black Fever era. HoloVision was the first consumer-entertainment application to hit the market, affecting only the user's visual faculties. The next phase was to find a way to reach the other senses. The culmination of those efforts was the LIFEstream system.
The LIFEstream system allows a user, known as a Conduit, to transmit their current lived experience to another user, known as a Receiver. The senses transmitted are the five external senses, or exteroception: vision, audition, taction, gustation, and olfaction. Receivers see, hear, feel, taste, and smell exactly what the Conduit does, as if their own bodies were the Conduit's.
Official Pitch versus Actual Use
The official pitch, written by Dal's marketing department, was aspirational: put yourself in the seat of your favorite athlete during the championship game, or your favorite artist playing their most iconic set, or your favorite racing driver pulling impossible turns. Experience the best of what human experience has to offer. Unofficially, the system worked just as well for broadcasting rocket launches, F1 driving, sexual assault from either perspective, robberies, real and near-death experiences, and pornography. The user's body, deceived by the incoming sense data, released the chemicals appropriate to the experience: dopamine, endorphins, adrenaline. The more extreme the experience, the heavier the chemical bath.
The first vision-only version was already addictive enough. The second, with audio, multiplied the addiction rate. The third and final iteration introduced tactile and movement-restriction modulation, and for many users at that point the non-virtual life was no longer worth living. Virtual streams operated as servers - a constructed avatar's experience broadcast to the receiver's body, available on demand, fictitious and metaphysical, escapable only by force of will.
The addicts became known as stream junkies. As addiction developed, users stopped maintaining their physical bodies. By 2084, deaths from dehydration, starvation, and untreated hygiene neglect among users who had not disconnected from their systems were common enough to be reported routinely. Public sentiment had long since stopped registering them as scandals.
Live Events and the Sports-Bar Stream
The hardcore-fan demographic favored live LIFEstreams over prerecorded content. The practice became the dominant way to watch professional sports. Portable LIFEstream rigs were carried into sports bars so aficionados could experience an event alongside their friends - typically with the stream's audio muted, so the user could hear the people physically next to them while seeing the game from the perspective of any player, coach, or referee on the field. The black-market live LIFEstream - unlicensed streams of underground events, with no rights cleared and chemical-output ceilings far higher than the legal versions allowed - became the most profitable corner of the industry. It is in this black market that X and his crew operate.
Bandwidth and the Cranial Port
The fundamental unit of neural information is the signal, and bandwidth is measured in signals per second (sps). Vision alone requires roughly 30 Gsps; HoloVision V1, vision-only, ran at about 50 Gsps. Adding audio brought V2 to about 85 Gsps. Adding tactile and movement-restriction modulation pushed V3 to about 200 Gsps. A full LIFEstream - the entire sensory existence of a Conduit - requires a minimum of 600 Gsps.
No wireless protocol of the era could carry even 300 Gsps, let alone 600. A full LIFEstream broadcast therefore demands a physical, deeply invasive Cranial Port installed at the base of the skull - the only connection capable of the bandwidth and the latency. HoloVision, needing a fraction of that throughput, never required one. This is the wall the Castañeda brothers hit: to broadcast streams out of X's own body, X needs the port, and neither the money nor the licensing from Dal or Cognis was ever within reach.

Neural Computation 
The Black Fever Pandemic had completely shut down the production of silicon processors. It was a field that required too many fragile supply chains to survive the disruption the Pandemic caused.

This stable and now only source of electricity would give them the power they needed, they just needed to figure out a way to use it to manufacture intelligent circuits. A French researcher that was previously working on keeping brain cells alive using just oxygenated blood and electricity was one of the lucky few to live in an underground lab. They were able to convince others using their research that you can grow, and based on the growth patterns you generate by changing the placement of the electricity, program brain cells into performing computations for you. That's just making them. They then proved that the Neural Organs they printed so to speak, could be programmed to perform arbitrary computations. This incredible breakthrough in biological computing allowed the researchers to have computers that are produced with electricity and the researchers own bio matter. The electricity also doubled as a solution to powering these new synthetic neural computers.
The lowest level of programming a neucomp is the growth itself. Neural stem cells are first grown from the DNA of existing brain cells, then coaxed into specific cell types - the rough analog of silicon deposition and etching. Cell-type conversion is driven by phased pulses of electromagnetic waves, focused onto particular depths of the tissue, "baking" the neucomp's circuitry layer by layer over time. This staged patterning is what gives a neucomp its functional topology before any high-level program is ever loaded.


Daemons
A Daemon is a lightweight neucomp grown from an individual's own brain tissue. Its original purpose was translation: turning raw, noise-saturated brain signals into higher-level concepts - the intent to raise an arm, the symbolic form of an equation, an abstract goal - and encoding commands back to the body. A key Project Xero finding made this personal by necessity: only neural hardware grown from a given person's own cells can decode and encode that person's signals. Hardware grown from anyone else's brain is useless for the task. This was first pursued as the basis for an auto-diagnostic that would let a brain "talk" to a computer and report its own infection status; it was never used to diagnose Black Fever, but it became one of the pillars that let neural computing take over after the pandemic.
What the original researchers did not anticipate was that the Daemons would talk back. A Daemon speaks to its host through a voice only the host can hear, acting as an extension of the mind and a gateway to the digital world by thought alone. It was the first proof that a form of life could be hosted on lab-grown neural hardware - the seed from which Eidolons and Therodolons grew. The name is borrowed from the old Greek notion of a personal spirit attached to a person as an advisor for good or ill.
Because a Daemon is grown from its host's own tissue and already sits in the loop between intent and action, it can be delegated direct control of a connected machine — most consequentially, a craft's flight systems. A pilot facing a maneuver whose acceleration would grey or black out a human can hand the controls to their Daemon, which flies the craft cleanly through forces no conscious person could withstand.
HoloVision Technology 
HoloVision is the tech that people use in the lifestream world, of the 2050s and beyond, as their primary interface with computational assets. First was punch cards, tape then keyboards and command lines then operating systems and GUIs to touch screen devices and spatial tracking of motion using LiDAR with AR/VR peripherals. The next step in human-computer interface is hijacking the visual system to add holographic assets to the users vision while tracking neural outputs to operate said Holo assets. 
        At first the only sense that HoloVision tech was able to tap into was vision. After came the addition of being able to send audio data neurally without actually using air to transmit sound and people’s eardrums. The last part of the system to be introduced was restriction of movement. This allowed applications running on HoloVision to create imaginary boundaries that the user’s body, without the user’s explicit awareness, that their body would not pass through. Imagine a HoloVision application that would imitate the functionality of a rotary phone. The user would see a holographic version of the rotary phone. The holo assets, in this case a rotary phone, would move and react to the user’s movements as if it was actually there. The added change was that now the body of the user itself would be restricted to respecting the space outlined by the hologram as if the Holo asset was a tangible physical object. This means the user's hands would stop their travel through the phone receiver as if an old school banana style receiver was actually in their hands. They would feel it too. To someone not using a HoloVision device with shared holo assets, it would look as though the HoloVision user was a mime pulling the perfect imitation of a person using a rotary phone. These three inputs into the senses would provide a level of immersion with the virtual world never before attained. He sold her into a labor indenture — a fixed term of bonded service in the United States, the cost of her crossing to be worked off on the far side — and he told himself it was mercy. A hard life in America, however unfree, was a longer life than the hunted one that waited beside him; sooner or later someone would come to collect on the family name, and he did not intend for her to be there when they did. In his mind he was trading her years of freedom for the plain fact of her survival. He thought it the last and hardest gift a father could give. He never told her she had been sold; she boarded believing she was being smuggled to safety.
She crossed the ocean her father had once commanded the way the powerless always cross it — stowed in cargo, in the cold and the fouled air and the thirst, weeks in the dark among others who had struck the same bargain. None of them knew the bargain was a lie. The pipeline that carried undocumented bodies across the Pacific did not deliver workers to any employer; there was no indenture, no term, no far side to work toward. It delivered raw material. Its real buyers were the black-market biomedical operations that needed what no consenting donor and no traceable corpse could supply — living organs, fresh tissue, and above all intact neural matter, taken from people no one would ever think to report missing. Her father had sold her years. The men he sold her to had bought her by the gram. She arrived in Southern California not as a laborer and not as a refugee but as inventory, freighted into an industrial district to be processed like everything else that came up the line.
Rescue and the Crew
She did not wait to learn what processing meant. In the confusion of the offload, somewhere between the cargo and the intake room, she got loose and ran — out into the dark warren of warehouses, scrap yards, and dead-automata lots that the operation had chosen precisely because no one watched it. That was where X and Adan crossed her path. They were not there for her; they were there, in 2078, to strip a biomedical lab of the equipment Adan needed for his drops work, and they found her instead — hiding in the sprawl, half-starved, barely alive. They got her out. She was eighteen; X was sixteen, Adan nineteen.
She recovered in the shop over the following weeks, and a month in X bought her a HoloVision V3 of her own. Once she had her strength back she came down to the garage basement and pitched the thing that turned their hustle into real money: a betting platform layered over X's underground LIFEstream races. The competing streamers ante into a winner-take-all pot and split a cut of ticket sales; what they don't know is that the book for the audience's wagers is built and run by Min and the Castañedas themselves. They don't fix the races — X wins them honestly, mostly — they simply are the house, and X takes a piece of every bet whether he wins or loses.


Deimos Vane
Origin
Deimos Vane is the only human clone on Earth and one of the most closely held secrets in the world - known to almost no one beyond Atticus Pallas, Deimos himself, and the small circle that grew and raised him. He is Atticus's physical vector: the body that walks in the open world and does what Atticus will not risk his own life to do. The clone exists for a single cold reason. Atticus, as exactly the clientele the Afterlife Program was built for, has already secured his own continuation as an Eidolon; his immortality is hosted, safe, and digital. The flesh copy is therefore expendable by design - an instrument, not an heir. The same logic MEGACORP used on the Athena crew, Atticus turned on his own blood.
He was forced to adulthood on an accelerated timeline, his body cultured and matured the way the world has learned to grow muscle and tissue for everything else. The public knows him as a 26-year-old prodigy born in 2058 - a clean paper trail of a life that was never lived. In truth he was created in 2078 and is, chronologically, six years old. A man's body, a manufactured past, and a loyalty to Atticus engineered as deliberately as the rest of him. He was raised to revere the man he is a copy of, which is precisely what makes his eventual turn cost him everything he was built to be.
Team Phaethon
Deimos races for MEGACORP's HRL entry, Team Phaethon - a name that quietly tells his whole story. In myth, Phaethon was the son of the sun-god Helios who begged to drive his father's chariot, lost control of it, and was struck down before he could scorch the Earth. In a league named for the sun itself, Atticus entered the son of the sun who falls from the sky. The public reads the name as triumphant solar branding; only Atticus knows it as a sentence already passed. The cruelty is doubled in the driver's own name: Deimos, the Greek personification of dread. Atticus named his disposable copy Dread and set him on a team named for the doomed son - both christenings private jokes only the maker is in on.
The Rivalry
For most of the season Deimos is X's open antagonist - crowding him on track in ways meant to kill rather than beat him, taunting him before races and in the press. The conflict is theater, and only two people know it: Deimos, who performs it on instruction, and Atticus, who authored it. X experiences it as real. The public devours it; the media inflate it into serialized fake news, manufacturing fresh threads of bad blood between races. Atticus lets all of it run, because a hated villain on the grid sells the spectacle he needs - and because a publicized feud is perfect cover for whatever "racing incident" he might one day need between the two of them.
The Driver
The horror beneath Deimos is that he was never entirely driving. A racer at this level is ported in - wired to his craft through a cranial connection at full LIFEstream bandwidth, his nervous system and the machine effectively one. That same channel is the attack surface. The LIFEstream system has, since HoloVision's third iteration, been able to impose movement restriction on a body without the user's awareness - to decide where a person's limbs will and won't go. With DAL's complicity, Atticus weaponized that capability into something total: through the system, he can seize a racer's controls the way an Eidolon takes control of an Anthromata through a Driver. In the moments that matter, that is exactly what Deimos is - a man with a face bolted onto an automaton's leash. He is, technically, Atticus's driver. The deepest dread of his life is that he cannot locate the seam: he does not know which of his sharpest saves and cruelest moves on track were his own and which were his maker's hand on the wheel. The same hack is the weapon eventually aimed at X.
The Turn and the Finale
        The change in Deimos is slow and almost involuntary. Underneath the performed hostility, something warms toward X across the season - a reluctant recognition, the only thing in his manufactured life he didn't choose to feel. Running against it is terror. Each race he nearly dies in is fresh proof that he is as expendable as anyone else on the grid, and that he can be driven into the danger without his consent. By the third race the dread has boiled over into open volatility - he lashes out at X and the whole field, not from malice anymore but from a panic he can't name. The private softening and the public unraveling are the same man: the real one surfacing while the built one comes apart.
In the Tartarus Run finale, Atticus seizes the controls and drives a racer into a deorbit burn. Deimos's climax is the inversion of everything he was made to be - he fights Atticus for the wheel of his own body and breaks the hack. The first fully autonomous act of his life is refusing his maker. He survives by it, and walks out to expose everything: the engineered death of Max Lauda, the DAL-assisted LIFEstream hack, the clone-raised-to-die scheme, and the Athena automata swap - the last of which clears Lazaro's name and reverses the blame MEGACORP used to gut the Castañeda settlement. The disposable man's confession is what finally indicts the man who made disposability a business.


Atticus Pallas
The Man No One Knows
Almost nothing about Atticus Pallas is publicly known - no confirmed age, no birthplace, no origin. He does not hold a public office in any Megacorp or sit for interviews; he operates through ownership and proxies, the shadow behind enterprises whose decisions he makes without ever appearing to. What is true of him is mostly measured rather than known: he controls on the order of eight percent of the world's wealth, which places him among the most powerful individuals alive. The obscurity is not an accident of the record. It is the condition that lets him act.
The Thesis
Atticus does not want humans in space. He wants machines - machines under his control - to be the ones that explore, colonize, and extract, so that the entire off-world economy answers to him. To get there, the public has to believe that humans simply cannot survive long-term spaceflight. His thesis, stated plainly: if they couldn't survive at home, how would we survive beyond the Moon and on Mars? It is a lie. He and his Cabal manufactured it to clear the field of human ambition and seize a colonial space economy for themselves - obfuscation and misdirection in the service of wealth-hoarding at a scale no single fortune could otherwise reach.
Athena and Lauda
Two engineered catastrophes bookend his campaign. The first was the death of Max Lauda - staged as a tragic accident decades ago, but in truth Atticus's doing, the match that killed the original Helios Racing League and put the public's appetite for space to sleep. The second was Project Athena. As the largest single investor and true owner of the Athena enterprise, Atticus ordered the automata swap that killed the crew - and then let the world read the disaster as proof of his thesis, that the mission had failed through human error. The revived Helios Racing League is the same instrument turned up to scale: a spectacle he funds for the express purpose of broadcasting humans dying at the controls, in space, again and again.
The Mandate He Cannot Break
For all his power, Atticus is beaten by a law he cannot touch. The disclosure mandate forces every Megacorp to keep showing the public its advances, including its space technologies, and every disclosure reignites the very dream he is trying to extinguish. He is not powerful enough to repeal it; no one is. So the dream never dies - he can only put it to sleep. It wakes again in a generation too young to remember Lauda, one that does not weigh the odds of death against the pull of knowledge. That generation is X's, and it is the thing Atticus's whole strategy cannot kill.
Deimos and the Eidolon
Atticus's own continuation is already secured: as exactly the clientele the Afterlife Program serves, he holds an Eidolon of himself, his immortality hosted and digital. That is precisely why he could afford to make Deimos disposable - a flesh clone to be his hand in the world while his real self waits, safe, in a rack. His reach into the races runs through the DAL-assisted LIFEstream hack, the weapon that lets him seize a driver's controls outright (see Deimos Vane). Everything about him resolves to the same principle: people, even his own copy, are instruments - and he made disposability a business.


Project Athena and the Etna Incident 
         X’s dad after the war and the era of black fever, is chosen and hired as a nuclear-electric engineer for humanity's most daring and dangerous adventure yet: land and mine an asteroid. Since humans first landed on the moon, man had yet to embark on such a momentous adventure. They called it the voyage of the century. The media coverage of the event was incessant. Even relatively irrelevant crew members such as X’s father were meticulously interviewed by the media. In one profile on him the home, workshop and land that had been awarded to him and his family was the environment in which the interview took place. A toddler and a young boy can be seen in the hands and laps of the brave ex-military engineer and future interplanetary traveler alongside his lovely wife. The toddler and the boy would later turn out to be X and his older brother, Adan. That would be one of the last times that the entire family was together. A memory that Adan would attempt to carry with him but eventually fade. X wouldn’t remember this moment at all and would only have the recording of the interview to remind him of this happy family moment. Close to the actual launch everything was going to plan. The ship carrying hundreds, 555 to be exact, took off from earth’s low orbit without a hitch. It would take a year and a half for the craft to accelerate and decelerate to its destination at the near edge of the asteroid belt. In the time between liftoff and arrival the crew that would take care of the ship while most of the crew slept in hibernation chambers, had plenty of free time to communicate with their family back home. At first live video calls were capable of being had but as the ship sped up and got further and further away from earth, the relativistic lag made it impossible to have normal conversations.. They would begin to send pre-recorded messages. It's through these messages that X would have his first memories of his father. Watching their father’s messages and recording their own became a ritual for the family. It would be a year and a half till the craft reached its intended target. From then on the mission would require the undivided attention of the crew. Even medical or family emergencies would be kept in a data-vault that awaited the crew once their return home had commenced. The crew was expected to work 12 hour shifts taking all of their time and energy. This was in order to extract as many materials as possible while consuming the least resources possible, those being time, food, water, etc. It was grueling work. Made all the more complicated by the required space suits. Though they had adequate ventilation, the wearer's mobility and dexterity was greatly hindered. The microgravity of the asteroid was also difficult to manage. Special consideration had to be taken on how the weight of the craft and its cardo was distributed. Two doors on opposite ends were required to empty the ship's contents. They made sure to extract nearly the same amount of tools and mining materials at the same time. Once they unloaded the necessary cargo the crew moved to building the refinement and habitation infrastructure required to extract the maximum amount of valuable material while bringing back as little weight possible and house the workers while they work. They would still sleep, eat and do all other activities as they had on their way to the asteroid on the ship. 
         It was during the end of their stay, when 95% of the material being returned by weight is on the craft and 1% of the total mining was left to be completed, with 1% in transit and 3% in processing that the processing plant exploded. The crew had overheated the plant by adding too much material to the smelter too quickly. Being in space on an asteroid requires very tight controls over the temperature, pressure and a mix of other things. Feeling in a rush and stressed by the living situations a miner over burdens the smelter's capacity and hellishly hot melted metal pours all over the chamber floor. This was the first time this had happened. Although the floor was made to withstand minor spills they hadn't assumed the temperature of the interior would be as high as it was considering it wasn't meant to run this long. Two unconnected engineering factors quickly become one as the metal reacts with the chamber interior chemicals and flash the chamber with smoke blowing out the chamber windows filling the smelting plant’s interior lab with toxic smoke. Some crew members run to put on their space suits, others stand stuck in shock and horror but it only takes one to almost instinctively open an emergency airlock breach and suck the air and everything out of the lab into the vacuum. Quickly other airlock breaches burst and the entire structure blew out. Nearly one half of the crew was gone in an instant. Those who were quick enough to put on their space suits were none the less thrown into space around the asteroid. To die of asphyxiation when their oxygen levels reach 0. The loneliest deaths in human existence. The only crew from the “day shift” that survived relatively untouched was the mining crew in the shafts. Oddly enough the most dangerous position turned out to be the safest. The crew of around 280 men now had a decision to make. They convened in the ship's last hall, the dining room, and stuffed into the still too small space, they had to vote on a plan. Either they quit the mission and head home with 95% of the job done. Or they stay and complete the remaining 5% of work with minimal infrastructure and tools over an extended period thanks to half of the remaining sources required to stay were now suddenly available. Intense deliberation and even fights break out in the heat of the public debate. The MEGACORP contacted them during this meeting as if they knew it was happening all along. They sweetened the option to stay even more by offering the remaining crew a 50% share of the deceased's cut. Since they had died now only 25% of the original amount will go to the widow and family as a sort of life insurance policy. As quickly as they barged into the meeting the MEGACORP communication cut out leaving the bewildered crew even more confused. Noticing that they were being watched they decided to calm down and put things to a vote with old school pencil and paper. A decision of 3 to 2 to leave passed. A lot of the crew cried in cheer while others expressed relief apprehensively. A rift in the crew had ripped through its ranks. A few groups began to splinter off into private conversations ranging from a call to recast their votes to stay all the way to mutiny. The MEGACORP picked up on all of this just as they had been from the beginning of the mission. They had gone as far to prepare and pay off their loved ones to keep them happy. Some weren't even legally married anymore. Back on earth but their now ex spouse was being paid by MEGACORP to keep the “marriage” going through their messages by acting as though everything was fine. Another poor crew member's family all died in a car crash. MEGACORP hired look-alike actors to keep the illusion that nothing had happened at all. In their minds it was in everyone's best interest if they maximized their returns by keeping as much as the crew happy. Of course they insisted upon themselves that it was also in the best interest of those crew they lied to for they would now have to share less of their wealth. 
Beginning of the end of the Mission: The decision makers in MEGACORP ground control were growing impatient. They had secured almost all of the processed material, yet they were anxious to increase their profit margins just a bit more and were unhappy with the remaining crew’s decision to leave. Sensing that they had lost all control over the crew through the subtle manipulation tactics they had employed throughout their entire journey. Atticus Pallas - the largest single investor in the Athena enterprise and its true owner - had had enough. While the other project heads had rescinded their desire for more profits in favor of the crew’s decision to return. Atticus had decided, without consulting anyone, to execute the backup plan. Many contingencies had been planned for years before the crew ever took off. Around the same time the first of the crew were being selected and trained, an autonomous cybernetic automaton had begun development. Using the vast amount of neural research and knowledge collected during the era of the black fever, the mega conglomerate formed after to create Earth Tower 1 MEGACORP began putting it together to test and ultimately create semi-biological automatons driven using synthetic brains and muscles. The plan was to stow these automatons in an inaccessible area of the craft. Since the human crew needed to be aware of the ship’s weight management throughout the entire mission it had to be accounted for in the a ship system no one could access but had to trust the MEGACORP was correct. The ship's radioactive waste management system, which was the 2nd furthest section from the crew quarters and the most dangerous, was the perfect place to situate them. While the automatons organic matter had encountered radiation damage due to its proximity to the fusion reactors and waste storage, they were declared to be capable of operating within the desired parameters. The automatons wouldn't require food nor sleep nor water like their human counterparts. However best for MEGACORP and its owners, they did not need to return to earth. Ditching the automatons and stripping the craft of the now useless materials needed to sustain the human crew would allow the automatons to pack the ship beyond its nominal rated return weight to 130%! Unknown to the other leaders, this had been Atticus's plan all along. He never intended on letting the human crew return back. Nor had he intended on paying the full sum to the crew and their families. It bugged him to think that the families would even get 25% of their share after losing their loved ones. The only reason that he had even allowed a human crew to attempt the mining operation was because his automatons were still too new and unpredictable to dependably rely on them to achieve the mission's objective.  What had changed to make them able to finish the remaining 5%+ of the mission? It was the fact that they had been reading the neural telemetry of the entire crew throughout the entire mission from their space and mining suits. This allowed the automaton lab to wrangle the complicated signals required to drive a human body and therefore the cybernetic functions of the muscle and computation cells/organs in the automaton. Helios Racing League
        Originally envisioned at the beginning of the reconstruction era, the original Helios Racing League was an effort to stoke enthusiasm for space exploration and exploitation. It aimed to do so by turning space technology from something that appealed to space aficionados to the lay man. It would create heroes that the public could rally behind and support in their sponsor corporations development of better materials, more powerful engines and other general technologies that would help the corporations in their quest to colonize the stars. It did not, however, make it past the development stage. This was due to the death of the world’s most acclaimed traditional racing pilot, Max Lauda, at the helm of a DFD craft during a public test run.
Lauda's fatal test run was, technically, a publicity stunt. That meant it had been filmed from every angle, by every outlet that mattered, in resolutions designed for broadcast. The footage proliferated in the days after his death - and the public's response startled the entertainment industry. A subculture of Lauda devotees and morbid spectators began trading and promoting the crash footage. The videos became, briefly, the most-watched content on Earth. People did not turn away. They returned, repeatedly, to watch a hero die at his moment of greatest vulnerability.
Sports executives took note. The lesson they drew was that an audience grieving the death of a celebrated figure was no different, commercially, from an audience hungry for the same spectacle from people they had no emotional investment in. A new generation of extreme sports rose through the 2050s and 2060s on this thesis. The participants were not stars. They were disposable players whose primary appeal was the certainty that some of them would not survive the season. By the time Promethean revived the Helios Racing League in 2084, the audience had been schooled in willful ignorance for nearly forty years. The dangerous parts were no longer a drawback; they were the product.
        In the time between Max Lauda’s death, traditional motorsports had waned in popularity, never reaching the same level of cultural relevance as it did before the BFP.  Granted to the popularity of extreme sports and their fusion with LIFEstream technology, Promethean, the owner of traditional motorsports companies, decided to bring the Helios Racing League back for a new audience. 
        Badly losing business and needing to make a splash, Promethean spared no expense selecting and building their new organization and facilities like testing ground and the race circuits themselves. Part of the massive expense was requiring the rights to race at the locations they chose. In the end, the first season of the revived HRL would consist of only 3 races: Star City Circuit at the base of ET1, Charon's Gauntlet at Port Charon at the top of the Space Fountain section, and the Tartarus Run held in Erebus, the deep-space region around the counterweight Atlas at geostationary altitude. There would be 16 teams with 16 drivers, each sponsored by a megacorp and the mega’s home nation. The drivers would represent the best that their country had to offer, while at the same time knowing the odds that they wouldn't survive. 
Helios Cup
The annual championship trophy of the revived Helios Racing League. The 2084 season consists of three races held at successively higher and more dangerous altitudes along Earth Tower 1.
Races:
1. Star City Circuit: The simplest of the three. Three laps around Star City's ring of 120 fusion reactors at the base of ET1. Pilots are given a 50m height ceiling to keep them inside the obstacle field rather than above it. Designed to introduce the season and the grid to the public.
2. Charon’s Gauntlet: Five laps around Port Charon, the industrial logistics city at the top of ET1's Space Fountain section. Pilots must thread incoming freight from the lunar mass driver - the "highway in the sky" delivering Processed Regolith - while navigating the orbital corridors around the station. The format earns its name from the cargo a pilot must dodge in real time.
3. Tartarus Run: The finale, run inside Erebus, the deep-space region surrounding ET1's counterweight at geostationary altitude. The circuit is man-made, carved from Processed Regolith blocks left over from the construction of Atlas, the counterweight that holds the entire elevator under tension. The most lethal race on the calendar.

Glossary 
* Afterlife Program: The commercial successor to Project Afterlife after the founding of Cognis. A legitimate Cognis division selling Eidolon hosting to the richest and most influential people alive - celebrities, politicians, and the ultra-wealthy.
* Anthromata: A more humanized variant of standard Automata designed for service roles such as waiter, usher, or secretary. Features a thinner human-like body, synthetic skin, a fully articulating human head and neck, and role-appropriate clothing.
* Atlas: The counterweight at the top of Earth Tower 1's Space Tether at geostationary altitude (36,000km). A compacted heap of Processed Regolith from the Moon, held under orbital tension. Named for the Greek Titan condemned to bear the weight of the heavens, because it is the load-bearing element that holds the entire space elevator upright. The Tartarus Run race is set in Erebus, the region surrounding it.
* Automata: Anthropomorphic robots developed during the Black Fever Era by the United Earth to perform hazardous, labor-intensive tasks without risk of infection. Consist of a metallic endoskeleton with lab-grown muscles and a synthetic brain called a Driver housed in the chest cavity. Immune to Black Fever due to having no blood or exposed brain tissue.
* Big One: Largest earthquake in California's history. Destroyed southern and central California in 2034. Opened Disaster Ridge and exposed rich Lithium-6 deposits.
* Bio-compute (bio-computing): The biological-neural-tissue substrate class and the industry built on it - the LIFEstream world's equivalent of "silicon" as both material and sector. Encompasses the growth, patterning, fabrication, and operation of neucomps. Distinct from neural computing, which is the discipline of running computation on that substrate.
* Black Fever (BF): An ultra-deadly, head-exploding virus that kills almost 900 Million people in the 2040s.
* Black Fever Nodules: Individual sites of BF infection where catalytic material is built and stored out of dead blood, neural tissue, and brain-blood proteins. Each nodule is part of the brain's Morticell Net. 
* Black Fever Pandemic (BFP): A global event during the spread of the virus until its cure and distribution.
* Black Fever Virus (BFV): The specific family of viruses that spawn out of the North Korean virus development program.
* Camp Oasis: Third designation of the Disaster Ridge military installation, following FOB Oasis. Marks the transition from a temporary forward operating base to a permanent fortified garrison with housing on the flattened bottom level of the Ridge.
* Cast Mare Regolith (CMR) / Lunar Marble: Acid-proof construction stone made from degassed, titanium-rich lunar mare regolith, the spoil of Helium-3 extraction, sintered or cast into a dense, near-black, marble-veined solid. Immune to acid rain owing to its silicate/titanium-oxide chemistry and vacuum-formed near-zero porosity. The highest-volume cargo carried down Earth Tower 1 and the bulk commodity underpinning its economy.
* Charon's Gauntlet: The second race of the Helios Cup season. Five laps around Port Charon at the top of Earth Tower 1's Space Fountain section. The format takes its name from the freight traffic pilots must dodge in real time - incoming Processed Regolith cargo from the lunar mass driver - which forms a literal gauntlet of obstacles.
* Cognis: The largest and only neucomp infrastructure operator - the world's builder and host of neucomps, and the toolmaker for programming them and growing their neurons. The "AWS" of the neural era. Host and seller of the Afterlife Program.
* Combat Outpost Isotope (COPI): Original name given to the marine outpost in Disaster Ridge following confirmation of Lithium-6 deposits after the Big One.
* Conduit: A LIFEstream user who transmits their lived sensory experience to one or more Receivers in real time
* Cranial Rupture: Technical medical name given to the explosion of people's heads as a result of the BFV. Official cause of death terminology.
* Cure Development: The phase of Project Xero spanning roughly 2042–2045, in which the Xero Network simultaneously developed neural tissue cultivation protocols, miniaturized brain scanning hardware, and the parallel antibody trials that produced the eventual Black Fever vaccine. The data overflow generated by these trials directly triggered the invention of the neucomp.
* Daemon:  A personal neucomp grown from an individual's own brain tissue that interprets their neural signals for other machines and speaks to them through a voice only they can hear. An extension of the mind that lets the user act on the digital world by thought — and, when delegated, fly a connected craft through g-loads that would incapacitate the human pilot. Pronounced "day-mon." The first demonstration that life could be hosted on neural hardware, and the technical ancestor of the Eidolon and Therodolon.
* D-He³ Reactor: The first practical fusion reactor, achieved by Dr. Bruce Deng's team on October 3rd, 2044. Nearly aneutronic, with a small neutron bleed from D-D side reactions requiring shielding. The backbone of fixed industrial power through the Reconstruction Era and beyond. Fuel: Deuterium and Helium-3.
* Direct Energy Conversion (DEC): A method of harvesting energy from charged fusion byproducts directly using electric and magnetic fields, bypassing thermal conversion entirely. Achieves efficiencies of 70–90% compared to roughly 35% for thermal cycles. Only viable with fully aneutronic reactions such as p-B¹¹.
* Direct Fusion Drive (DFD): A type of fusion-power based propulsion where the plasma from the fusion reaction itself can be used to propel a craft.

Hosted by Cognis on their servers and able to take control of Anthromata through custom Drivers. Sold through the Afterlife Program to the richest and most influential people alive - celebrities, politicians, the ultra-wealthy - but to a clientele so small, and so officially unacknowledged, that to the general public the technology is little more than rumor. * Helios Racing League (HRL): Dangerous sports racing league for next-generation miniaturized DFD equipped spacecraft.
* Helios Cup: The annual championship trophy of the Helios Racing League. The 2084 season consists of three races held at successively higher and more dangerous altitudes along Earth Tower 1: the Star City Circuit at the base, Charon's Gauntlet at Port Charon, and the Tartarus Run in Erebus. Winning the Helios Cup requires surviving the Tartarus Run.
* Helium-3 (He-3): The isotope used as fuel in D-He³ fusion reactors. Extremely rare on Earth but deposited continuously on the lunar surface by the sun. The most sought-after material in the world following the fusion breakthrough of 2044. Control of its supply chain is synonymous with control of global energy. NovaTech's empire is built on owning it.
* HoloVision: Neural signal manipulating device that stimulates sight, vision and sound to create an interface for personal computing.
* Lazy Black Fever: A slower-acting variant of the Black Fever Virus that progresses gradually enough to avoid detection by standard screening for years. Symptoms progress from headaches to migraines, chills, dizziness, seizures and eventual fever before Cranial Rupture. Ines Castañeda was among its last known carriers.

* MEGACORP: A Syndicated Megacorporation comprising the top five largest global companies. Funded and built Earth Tower 1 and organized Project Athena.
* Megacorporation (Mega(s)): A state sanctioned but not necessarily owned corporation that specializes in one specific business horizontal at a global scale.
   * Horizontal: The standard Megacorporation.
   * Monolithic: A megacorporation that has expanded out of its strictly business horizontal into vertical solutions as well.
   * Parasitic: Corporations that exist between new niches that Megacorps cannot currently fill.
   * Syndicated: A Mega that is composed of many H-Megas.
* Morticell: A localized structure of dead material formed by the Black Fever Virus at a single infection site within the brain's capillary system. Built from the carcasses of asphyxiated blood cells along with neural tissue debris and brain-blood proteins. Catches healthy cells until pressure causes the surrounding capillary to clot or rupture.
* Morticell Net: The communication and coordination network linking all of an individual's Morticells together. Formed by the Black Fever Virus through hijacking the host's neural pathways, it synchronizes the growth rates of each Morticell and triggers the simultaneous rupture of every infection site in the cascade event known as Cranial Rupture.
* Neucomp: A synthetic neural computer. The device class was born during Project Xero through the collaboration of three national contingencies - French researchers at CERN who discovered electrically patterned neural-tissue growth, a Dutch precision-engineering team who built the first reproducible patterning equipment, and an Italian sub-group at Gran Sasso who integrated the two into a working signal processor in early 2044. Created out of necessity when the antibody-trial data load overwhelmed Project Xero's collapsing silicon stockpiles. Pronounced "new-comp.”
* Neucomp Stack: The three-layer technology stack underlying all post-pandemic bio-compute. Substrate (French in origin, modern home Sanofi Bio); patterning tooling (Dutch in origin, modern home Eindhoven Lithos); architecture and integration (Italian in origin, modern home Sapienza Neurotecnica). Industrial fabrication post-Reconstruction migrated to Tessera Photonics in the East Asian Alliance; operations and rental concentrated under Cognis in the United States.
* Neural Computing (neucomputing): The discipline and practice of running computation on neural hardware (neucomps) rather than silicon. "Neural computation" refers to the process itself.
* Neural Organ: Synthetically grown, multi type brain cell organs used for computation. Some cell types are more adequate for some computations than others. Like an FPGA of brain parts with different functions.
* Neuro-Optic Stabilizer Solution (NOSS): An antipsychotic medication developed in the Reconstruction Era to treat schizophrenic hallucinations, post-traumatic trances, phantom pain and other psychosomatic conditions resulting from Black Fever trauma. Administered as eye drops taken at least once daily. Known colloquially as Drops or Gotas.

An amended version of NSPD-51 enacted during the Black Fever Era that replaced the traditional continuity-of-government structure with a streamlined scientific congress of 435 researcher-representatives empowered to make rapid decisions alongside the President. The blueprint of United Earth governance.

Designed by Lazaro Castañeda under a NovaTech-sponsored doctorate (completed 2052); credited wholly to the company, which built the first commercial unit in 2053 and took it to be the world's first — unaware he had secretly hand-built a working unit, the genesis reactor, over a year earlier.
* Port Charon: Industrial logistics city built at the top of Earth Tower 1's Space Fountain section, 2,000km above Star City. Named for Charon, the ferryman of the Greek underworld, because it is the transshipment hub where freight crosses between earth-bound traffic and the deep-space cargo arriving on the lunar "highway in the sky." A working-class port city named by its workers rather than by corporate marketing. Hosts Charon's Gauntlet, the second race of the Helios Cup season.
* Processed Regolith (PR): Compacted lunar regolith used as counterweight mass for Earth Tower 1's tether. Manufactured on the Moon and launched via mass driver toward GEO, creating what workers called a highway in the sky, where it is accreted onto the Atlas counterweight. PR is the counterweight grade of lunar regolith, selected and compacted purely for bulk mass, with no regard to mineralogy, and is distinct from Cast Mare Regolith (CMR), the building grade drawn from the same lunar mining stream but chosen for its titanium-rich, sour-rain-resistant composition, cast or sintered rather than merely compacted, and sent down the elevator to Earth rather than up to GEO. PR rides up; CMR comes down.

Officially part of the cure effort but concealed from the public and most lab staff. The mechanism by which the future Megacorp owners were promised digital immortality in exchange for consenting to the mergers; responsible for scanning their brains during the pandemic.
* Project Athena: MEGACORP's mission to mine asteroid Etna at the near edge of the asteroid belt and return rare earth minerals to Earth. Launched 2070, crew presumed dead 2072 following the Etna disaster.

* Project Xero: The single most important operation in human history enacted by the UEA - to cure Black Fever.

* Receiver: A LIFEstream user who receives and experiences the transmitted sensory output of a Conduit in real time.

* Sour Rain: Corrosive, faintly radioactive rainfall that became a permanent feature of Earth's climate after the North Korean salted bomb. As fallout settled into the lower atmosphere, its radiation fixed atmospheric nitrogen into nitric acid while the same particles were scavenged into the rain itself - acidic and radioactive at once. Named "sour" because no older word covered both: water gone acidic and gone bad. Caused solely by the fallout, not by industry, and so unaffected by the fusion transition. Arrives in discrete, drifting storms concentrated over cities; dissolves carbonate stone and concrete and chars exposed organic matter. Onset ~2042, matured by the early 2050s, declining over centuries with the orbital debris.
* Space Fountain: An active-support structure that uses magnetically accelerated ferrous material traveling through a vacuum tube to create rigidity and lift. The lower 2,000km section of Earth Tower 1 uses this design. Requires continuous, enormous power input to remain standing.
* Space Tether: A passive-support structure held aloft by the centrifugal tension of a counterweight in geostationary orbit. Forms the upper section of Earth Tower 1, from 2,000km to 36,000km. Limited by material tensile strength and anchor placement constraints.
* Spyder Tow Vehicle: A large multipedal robotic transport platform originally developed by a USC robotics lab as a tank chassis. Adapted after the Big One to tow disabled vehicles through the destroyed and irregular streets of Southern California. Adopted by the state government, commercialized rapidly, and instrumental in clearing the roads of Los Angeles in the disaster's aftermath.
* Star City: Final designation of the Disaster Ridge site, following Oasis City. The name adopted after MEGACORP assumed full control of the region following the completion and opening of Earth Tower 1 in 2066, reflecting its transformation into a global aerospace and economic epicenter.
* Star City Circuit: The opening race of the Helios Cup season. Three laps around Star City's ring of 120 fusion reactors at the base of Earth Tower 1, with a 50m altitude ceiling to keep pilots inside the obstacle field rather than above it. The simplest of the three races on the calendar, designed to introduce the season's drivers to the public.
* Tartarus Run: The third and final race of the Helios Cup season. Held inside Erebus, the deep-space region surrounding Atlas at geostationary altitude. The circuit is man-made, carved from Processed Regolith blocks left over from Atlas's construction. The most lethal race on the HRL calendar.
* Therodolon: The scientific term for a virtual copy of an animal's brain captured at a single point in time. Lives in a rack like an Eidolon and can take control of any Automata variant through an adequate Driver. Achieved early in Project Afterlife but never commercialized - a hosted animal mind offered no advantage over a mindless Automaton, which performs the same labor without the cost or complexity of sustaining a consciousness. To the public, Therodolons - like Eidolons - are the stuff of rumor: whispered about, officially unacknowledged, with no way to confirm they exist at all.
* Two-Reactor Doctrine: The principle by which the world's energy and propulsion economies operate on two distinct fusion technologies: D-He³ for large-scale fixed industrial power and p-B¹¹ for crewed spacecraft propulsion and power. Each reactor type is suited to a different application and era.
* United Earth (UEA): Black Fever Era and beyond replacement to the United Nations. Exerts much more power over governments compared to its predecessor by the need for mass, global-scale cooperation.
* Xero Network: The global communication and logistics backbone of Project Xero, connecting all Underground Labs across UEA member nations with unprecedented openness to coordinate the effort to cure Black Fever.
