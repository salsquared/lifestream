Aims and Scope
All good science fiction is grounded in reality but does not strictly adhere to it. I want to explore what the future of space planes might look like in the 2080s after certain technological developments have taken place and proliferated my world’s markets. To do so I need to first read the history of these technologies and where they currently are to see where they might go in the future.

Scientific Rationale
Fusion Reactors


Standard Chemical Notation


$$ X_{Z}^{A} $$ where 
* A is the Total Number of Nucleons; Protons + Neutrons, 
* Z is the Total Number of Protons => $$ A - Z = $$ The number of Neutrons
* X is the Chemical Symbol of the Element


A change in Z is what denotes a change in element.


For the highest levels of electricity conversion we want to focus on reactions that are aneutronic. This is since we want to only deal with reactions that don't require lots of shielding and are therefore light and don't pose a threat to the people around them. In addition we want to produce as much energy as possible with as little energy loss as possible which is what happens anytime we have to convert one type of energy (thermal in the case of neutronic reactions) to electrical energy. 
The Two-Reactor Doctrine


The world operates on two distinct fusion reactor technologies developed in sequence, each suited to a different application and era.
D-He³ — Large-Scale Industrial Reactor (BF Era)


$$ D + {}^{3}_{2}He \rightarrow {}^{4}_{2}He + p + 18.3\ \text{MeV} $$


The D-He³ reaction was the fusion breakthrough achieved by Dr. Bruce Deng's team on October 3rd, 2044, developed under extreme pressure in the underground research laboratories of the BF Era. The underground environment made the reactor's one significant drawback — a small neutron bleed (~5%) from unavoidable D-D side reactions — essentially irrelevant, as the surrounding rock and reinforced concrete provided passive shielding at no engineering cost.


D-He³ delivers the highest energy yield of any practical fusion reaction. It is nearly aneutronic, meaning its primary reaction produces no neutrons, though the minor D-D side reactions require some degree of shielding. At industrial scale, this shielding cost is a trivial line item. Future generations of this reactor technology are what power everything through the Reconstruction Era and beyond — ET1's reactor farm, lunar base grids, and megablock city infrastructure all run on D-He³ descendants.


NovaTech's empire was built on owning this technology and the He³ supply chain that feeds it. Because He³ must be extracted from the lunar regolith, control of lunar mining operations is synonymous with control of global energy.


Key properties
* Highest absolute energy output of any practical fusion reaction
* Nearly aneutronic (~5% neutron bleed from D-D side reactions)
* Requires shielding — viable for fixed industrial infrastructure, not crewed spacecraft
* Fuel: Deuterium (abundant) + Helium-3 (rare; lunar-sourced)
p-B¹¹ — Spacecraft Propulsion & Power Reactor (Reconstruction Era)


$$ p + {}^{11}_{5}B \rightarrow 3\ {}^{4}_{2}He + 8.7\ \text{MeV} $$


A decade after D-He³ matured, engineers scaling up crewed space operations hit a hard wall: adequate D-He³ shielding mass was incompatible with any practical crewed spacecraft design. The motivation to solve p-B¹¹ ignition — which requires plasma temperatures roughly three times higher than D-He³ — came directly from this constraint.


The p-B¹¹ reaction is fully aneutronic. All products are charged alpha particles (helium nuclei), which means their kinetic energy can be harvested directly via electromagnetic fields using Direct Energy Conversion (DEC) — no thermal cycle, no steam turbines, no heat exchangers. This yields theoretical efficiencies of 70–90%, versus ~35% for thermal conversion. Crucially, with zero neutron output, no shielding is required. The crew can sit meters from the reactor.


The first application was purely propulsion via the Direct-Fusion-Drive (DFD) engine. Power generation followed as the technology miniaturized through the 2060s–2070s.


Boron is abundant on Earth, which means p-B¹¹ reactor fuel cannot be monopolized the way He³ can. This is a meaningful crack in NovaTech's energy dominance and a key reason independent spacecraft operators can exist.


Key properties
* Fully aneutronic — zero neutron output, zero shielding required
* All charged particle products → pure Direct Energy Conversion (DEC)
* DEC efficiency: 70–90% (vs. ~35% for thermal conversion)
* Lower absolute energy output than D-He³, but unmatched power-to-mass ratio with no shielding overhead
* Fuel: Hydrogen (abundant) + Boron-11 (abundant; Earth-sourced)


Direct Energy Conversion (DEC)


In a standard neutronic fusion reactor, neutrons carry most of the energy out as heat. That heat drives a thermal cycle — boiling water, spinning a turbine — to generate electricity. This intermediary conversion is inherently inefficient (~30–40%).


In an aneutronic reaction, the fusion products are charged particles. Because they carry an electric charge, their kinetic energy can be captured directly using electric and magnetic fields — no heat exchange required. This is Direct Energy Conversion (DEC). It is the mechanism that makes p-B¹¹ reactors viable for spacecraft and is the reason the world's space propulsion economy is not simply a scaled-down version of its industrial energy economy.
Fusion Technology Timeline
* Oct 3rd, 2044 
   * Dr. Bruce Deng's team achieves stable D-He³ plasma confinement in the underground BF Era laboratories. The breakthrough is driven by the computational power demands of Project Xero.
* ~2048–2050 
   * First commercial D-He³ reactors come online. NovaTech consolidates a monopoly over He³ extraction and reactor manufacturing. The Reconstruction Era's energy backbone is established.
* ~2058
First successful p-B¹¹ ignition achieved, initially considered impractical due to the immense plasma temperatures required to sustain the reaction at small scale.
* 2062
   * Miniaturized p-B¹¹ Direct-Fusion-Drive (DFD) engine certified for crewed spacecraft. The technology that makes Project Athena's Enceladus mission possible.
* ~2065–2070
   * p-B¹¹ becomes the standard for all crewed spacecraft propulsion. D-He³ remains the undisputed backbone of fixed industrial power.
