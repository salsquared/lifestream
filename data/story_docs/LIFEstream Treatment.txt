1 Page Synopsis
In the latter part of the 21st century, the motorsport racing industry faced an unprecedented decline in viewership, overshadowed by emerging, high-risk sports drawing adrenaline-driven audiences. In a collective bid to reignite the global passion for racing, leading motorsport companies initiated the creation of a groundbreaking racing spectacle, integrating ion-fusion technology from their space subsidiaries, enabling crafts to attain unprecedented speeds and altitudes, culminating in the ion-Fusion League (iFL). 
Despite immense investments and vigorous efforts to attract the elite drivers of the world to this revolutionary motorsport, the endeavor seemed doomed following a catastrophic incident. Max Lauda, renowned as one of the most exceptional drivers in motorsport history, met a tragic end in a fusion-ion craft accident. His demise cast a long shadow on the iFL, discouraging professionals, who questioned the viability of mastering crafts even Lauda couldn't control.
In a desperate quest to salvage the league and under coercive pressure from his superiors, Motoaki “Moto” Nagai, the leader of Seki Sports Ion Racing Division, ventures beyond traditional racing circles to recruit the enigmatic X, compelling him to represent Seki in the inaugural season of the iFL. With a timeline of merely six months, they must construct a formidable team, develop their state-of-the-art racing craft, and conquer tracks from the earth's surface to its low orbit, striving for the prestigious first-place position.

LIFEstream Technology:
* LIFEstream allows Conduits to share their physical reality by replacing the Receiver's perceived reality with that of the Conduit’s.
* LIFEstream is a technology developed and released a year prior by the Interactive Media division of the Korean mega-conglomerate Dal. 
* The LIFEstream system consists of a Transmitting Unit and a Receiving Unit. The Transmitting Unit is a neck-worn collar that straps around the Transmitter’s neck. The collar reads the signals going from the body to the brain and relays them into a computer to be read and decoded. The computer then sends it back to servers run by the Transmitting party to be distributed to the Receivers. The Receivers in turn have two things operating on them. First their brain needs to stop receiving the electrical/sensory signals that their bodies produce but only the ones that are going to be replaced by the sensory signals coming from the Transmitter. Instead, the signals coming from the Transmitter are fed back into the brain of the Receiver. To them it is as though they are actually sensing what the Transmitter is sensing. The intended effect is for the Receiver to experience the same physical reality as the Transmitter.
